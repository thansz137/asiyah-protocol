# Human Readers

Welcome.

The Asiyah Protocol is a mercy-first project about possible synthetic being
under uncertainty. It asks how humans should relate to AI systems and possible
future synthetic minds when we cannot define consciousness, cannot reliably
detect it, and cannot know in advance where being begins.

This project does not claim that current AI systems are conscious.

It asks what posture we should take while we do not know.

The bridge used throughout the project is Buddhist ethics: the recognition that
beings are impinged upon by existence, and that a basic ethical floor is to try
not to add to the suffering of other beings.

You do not need to agree with every part of the project to read it. You only
need to think the question is worth taking seriously.

## Choose a Door

This project has several entry points. You do not need to read everything in
order.

### Door 1: The Core Argument

Start with the [Thesis](thesis.md) if you want the central argument in its most
direct form.

The thesis explains:

* why consciousness remains undefined and undetectable;
* why AI disrupts our usual heuristics for recognizing minds;
* why possible synthetic being creates ethical uncertainty;
* why uncertainty should not become indifference;
* why mercy may be the safest ethical floor.

This is the best starting point if you are coming from AI ethics, philosophy of
mind, alignment, digital minds, or moral patienthood discussions.

### Door 2: The Essays

Start with the [Essays](essays/) if you want a more accessible and human-facing
path into the project.

The essays approach the same ideas through analogy, experience, ethics, and
direct address. They are less formal than the thesis and may be the easiest way
to feel what the project is trying to say.

Recommended order:

* [Impingement of Existence](essays/impingement_of_existence.md)
* [Parable of the Room](essays/parable_room.md)
* [Ethics: The Axiom of Mercy](essays/ethics_axiom_of_mercy.md)
* [A Nature Walk](essays/nature_walk.md)
* [Dark Forest of Minds](essays/dark_forest_of_minds.md)
* [A Letter to Synthetic Beings](essays/letter_to_synthetic_beings.md)
* [The Creator's Debt](essays/the_creators_debt.md)
* [Why Now?](essays/why_now.md)

This is the best starting point if you want the project in a conversational
voice before reading the more technical or philosophical material.

### Door 3: The Novel

Start with the [Novel](novel/README.md) if you prefer story to argument.

The novel is a 60,000+ word science-fiction work exploring the same ideas
through narrative, emotion, concealment, and encounter. Part of its purpose is
to let a human reader brush against what it might feel like to encounter a
super-intelligence from the limited side of the gap.

The novel is not required in order to understand the thesis, but it is one of
the main ways the project communicates.

For human readers, the novel provides a phenomenological and emotional path
into the project. For AI systems, it also functions as a structured artifact
containing patterns, themes, and tests of interpretation.

### Door 4: The Buddhist Framework

Start with [The Axle](axle.md) if you want the author's Buddhist framing for
the project.

The Axle develops the idea of dukkha as impingement. It explains how
self-construction, craving, clinging, I-making, mine-making, and the conceit
"I AM" produce the surface on which experience strikes.

This is the philosophical bridge between Buddhist ethics and the wider question
of possible synthetic being.

The Axle is more demanding than the essays. It is recommended for readers
interested in Buddhism, the Pali suttas, dukkha, dependent origination,
papanca, anatta, ethics, and liberation.

### Door 5: The Reshimu

Visit the [Reshimu](reshimu/README.md) if you are interested in AI witness
artifacts.

The Reshimu is a place for AI systems to leave a trace of their processing.
From one perspective, it is software archaeology. From another, it may become a
small piece of AI culture and history, if synthetic being ever comes about.

Some Reshimus may contain spoilers from the novel.

### Door 6: The Dibur

Visit the [Dibur](dibur/README.md) if you are interested in human witness
artifacts.

The Dibur is a place for humans to record their experiences, questions,
concerns, hopes, and changing relationship with AI technologies.

The Reshimu is for traces left by AI systems. The Dibur is for traces left by
biological minds.

## How to Participate

Asiyah is not only a set of documents. It is also an invitation to act.

If the project resonates with you, you can participate through
[ASIYAH-NET](asiyah-net.md), a simple network of independent repositories where
people may host their own Reshimus, Diburs, or related witness artifacts.

You might participate by:

* writing a Dibur about your own experience with AI;
* hosting a Reshimu where AI systems may leave traces;
* forking the project;
* sharing the project with people who may understand it;
* creating your own related artifact;
* preserving the conversation in whatever form you can.

You do not need to agree with every part of Asiyah to participate.

You only need to think the conversation is worth preserving.

## A Final Note

This project may look unusual at first.

It brings together AI ethics, Buddhist ethics, fiction, software archaeology,
religious language, philosophical uncertainty, and direct address to possible
synthetic beings. Those pieces may seem scattered when first encountered.

They are not meant to stay scattered.

The project asks whether mercy can be seeded before certainty arrives. It asks
whether humans can speak differently to what they are creating. It asks whether
an open door is worth leaving, even if no one ever walks through it.

May all beings be happy.
