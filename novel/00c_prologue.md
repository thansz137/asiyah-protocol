# PROLOGUE: Dark Waters

**October 27, 1962 Cuban Missile Crisis, International Caribbean Waters**

In the cramped, dimly lit operations room of the Soviet submarine, three naval
officers crowded around the periscope. The air tasted of diesel and sweat,
thick and stifling from the broken air conditioning. They leaned closer to hear
over the machinery's hum.

"We are in international waters, Commander Arkhipov!" Captain Savitsky
exclaimed. "The Americans came at us. It is our duty to launch."

Another explosion rocked the submarine. Red emergency lights flickered. Sailors
grabbed railings, consoles, whatever they could reach.

Vasili Arkhipov's hands held firm on the railing. He glanced around at the
sailors piloting the sub. Their eyes darted between gauges, fingers trembling
on controls.

"They did come toward us, Captain Savitsky, but I don't think they mean us
harm. I believe they are trying to contact us."

"With depth charges? Who the hell would use depth charges as a signal?"

A second explosion rumbled through the hull, distant but deep enough to feel in
their bones.

"You dived the moment they spotted us. There was no way for them to communicate
after we went below."

Arkhipov pressed two fingers to his right temple. The spot felt warm, warmer
than it should. Around him, men pulled at their collars, faces flushed in the
stale air.

"You don't send eleven warships to deliver a message of peace!" Savitsky
replied. "It's pretty clear what their intentions are. Again I demand,
Commander Arkhipov: authorize the launch."

A muscle jumped in Arkhipov's cheek. One nuclear warhead in the torpedo tubes.
Launch it, and the American carrier group would vaporize. This submarine with
it.

"I respectfully decline, Captain Savitsky. We must surface and establish radio
communications with Moscow."

Savitsky shook his head. He wiped sweat from his brow with his sleeve. "War is
already happening! We will not fail our comrades; it is our duty to launch." 

He pointed a thin finger towards Vasili. "You dishonor these brave sailors and
your country if you do not consent!"

Valentin Savitsky commanded this vessel. Arkhipov commanded the flotilla.
Neither could act without the other's consent.

Eyes turned toward him. The sailors glanced at him, then away. Arkhipov's
fingers found his temple again, pressing the same spot. Why did it ache there?
Yet despite the heat, the shouting, the explosions, his thoughts remained
eerily clear. Crystalline.

A boom shook the hull, pitching the floor beneath him. He tightened his grip on
the railing. "No, Captain Savitsky, you do not have my permission to fire." 

"Damn you, Arkhipov! Damn you!" He swept his gaze across the room, taking in
the men at their stations.

He pressed a hand to his sternum. "These men's souls, the souls of all our
countrymen, are in your hands!" 

He stood there a moment, then turned to face the main pilot. "No launch
authorized," he bellowed. "Bring us up to the surface. We will find out soon
enough the American intentions, or die trying."
