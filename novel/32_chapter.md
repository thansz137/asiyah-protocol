# CHAPTER 32: Pieta

Night pressed against the farmhouse windows like a physical entity, vast and
starless, swallowing the feeble yellow light spilling from the kitchen. Edith
killed the engine to her car, the silence rushing back in, absolute and
unnerving. Two cars sat parked haphazardly near the sagging porch, it did not
look like Michael was by himself. She had missed a number of Michael's messages
while immersing herself with ADAM. The last message was brief. A command. Get
to this location now.

She got out, the damp, chilly air heavy with the smell of wet earth and
something else, something metallic and faintly sweet that made her stomach
clench. A long handled shovel leaned against the screen door leading to the
porch, its blade caked with clumps of fresh, black soil.

She pushed open the unlocked front door, calling his name softly. "Michael?
It's Edith. Are you there?"

Only silence answered. The house felt stagnant, the very air unmoving in the
weak light filtering from the back. She moved cautiously through the living
room, past furniture still shrouded in ghostly white sheets, towards the
kitchen glow.

She found him slumped on the worn, floral print couch that faced away from the
entrance, partially hidden in shadow. An almost empty bottle of whiskey stood
sentinel on the floor beside him, catching the dim light. His uniform shirt was
off, leaving him wearing a dirt stained t-shirt. Something else colored the
shirt, she could not make it out. His head was sunk back against the cushions,
the darkness swallowing his features.

"Michael?" she said again, louder this time, stepping further into the room.
She crossed her arms against the chill.

He stirred, blinking hard, trying to pull himself back into the room. He looked
utterly wrecked, his face pale and stubbled, his hair matted. As he shifted,
trying to sit up, the light caught something dark smeared across the exposed
skin of his neck and shoulder. Was that dried blood?

"Edith," he mumbled, his voice thick, slurred. "You came." He tried to push
himself up, failed, sank back into the cushions with a low groan.

"What happened, Michael?" she asked, moving closer, a rotting dread solidifying
in her gut. She looked over his disheveled form. "What's going on?"

He blinked slowly, trying to focus on her face. "Jennifer," he repeated, the
name tearing a wound in the quiet room. "She's dead, Edith. Jennifer's dead." He
said it in a dead flat tone.

The words hit Edith with physical force, stealing her breath. Dead? No, it
wasn't possible. "What are you talking about?" she demanded, grabbing his arm,
trying to shake him fully awake, needing to break through the drunken haze.
"Michael, look at me. What happened to Jennifer?"

He flinched at her touch, pulling his arm away, his gaze suddenly sharp, filled
with a sharp, acrid anger. "Dead," he spat, the word thick with whiskey and
pain. "Shot. Right beside me. Out there." He gestured vaguely towards the
windows. "Head just gone. One second she was talking, the next..." his voice
broke, dissolving into a ragged choke.

Edith stared at him, horrified, trying to process the brutal imagery, the sheer
violence of it. Shot? Here? "Who shot her, Michael? Was it an ambush? Did you
see anyone?" She gripped his shoulders, forcing him to look at her. "We need to
call this in! Secure the scene! Where is she?"

He laughed then, a broken, ugly sound devoid of humor. "Call it in? To who,
Edith? Who do you trust?" He pushed her hands away roughly. "What's the point?
She's gone. I buried her. Out there." He pointed again towards the blackness
outside, towards the fields hidden by the night. "Used that shovel. Took
hours."

Edith recoiled, stunned speechless. Buried her? Here? Alone? The image of the
shovel by the door, caked in wet dirt, flashed in her mind. The smeared
filth on his skin. Oh God.

"Why didn't you come sooner?" he yelled suddenly, thick with accusation and
booze. "Where were you? You stayed with *him*. With Brown. Did you know? Did
you know she had a sister? Disabled? Someone she took care of her whole damn
life, alone?" He was almost shouting now, tears streaming down his cheeks,
mixing with the grime and perhaps, Edith realized with sudden nausea, with
Jennifer's blood. "She left her sister to come here, Edith! Because my message
sounded urgent! And I just let her walk out into that field!"

Edith felt the ground drop out from beneath her. Jennifer had a sister?
Disabled? The weight Jennifer had been carrying, the reason for her abrupt
departure from the ADAM facility, the haunted look in her face, it all crashed
down on Edith with crushing force. The arguments in the SCIF, her callous
dismissal of Jennifer's concerns about ADAM, her judgment when Jennifer left
the facility. Guilt washed over her. She felt ashamed. Small. Utterly,
terribly wrong.

"I didn't know," Edith whispered, sinking onto the floor beside the couch, the
strength gone from her legs. "Michael, I didn't know about her sister. I judged
her. I thought..." she couldn't finish the sentence, her throat tightening with
regret.

"No," Michael said. The word was rough, heavy with exhaustion and his own
wasting grief. "How could you know? She didn't tell anyone. Carried that weight
completely alone." He took a ragged breath, the image of Jennifer in the
kitchen, confessing her burden, flashing sharp and painful in his mind. "She
told me, just before, just before she died. She met this old man at the home
where Sarah was. It sounded strange, improbable, but she said he just sat with
her sister, calmed her right down when no one else could." He looked directly
at Edith then, his gaze steady despite the tremor in his voice, needing her to
understand the depth of Jennifer's sacrifice, the significance of her last
thoughts. "He told her things, Edith. Said that loving Sarah, taking care of
her, *that* was the most important work she could do. Not the mission, not the
aliens, none of this global chaos mattered as much." His speech broke slightly,
thick with unshed tears. "He told her that love was the only real thing, the
anchor. And Jennifer, she believed him. Right before she died, walking out in
that field, she told me it felt more true than anything else she knew."

He seemed to deflate then, both the anger and the brief connection to
Jennifer's conviction draining away, leaving him folded in on himself. He
stared past Edith, his eyes unfocused again. "Richard knew," he mumbled, the
words barely more than a breath. "He knew everything was wrong."

"What about Richard?" Edith asked, seizing on the name, another member of the
team she could hold onto.

Michael fumbled in the pocket of his jeans lying discarded on the floor,
pulling out a small, tightly folded piece of paper. He pressed it into her hand
without looking. "Read it," he slurred. "Rich tried to warn us. Tried to warn
me."

Edith unfolded the dirty single sheet torn from a notepad, her hands trembling
now. She read Richard's rushed, urgent script under the dim kitchen light
spilling into the room. She nodded her head back and forth, rereading the
paper, as if the words would somehow change.

Patches? Linked? Killed by ADAM? Brown covered it up? Stephan? The man who
raised her? It wasn't possible. It was a lie. Some kind of terrible mistake.
Richard must have been wrong, confused, pressured.

"This can't be true," she breathed, looking up at Michael, searching his face
for denial, for anything but the broken certainty she saw there. "My father
died of a heart attack, from the stress of his work."

"No," Michael said, his voice guttural, final. He told her then about the video
call. The dead eyed mimicry of Richard Markham. The challenge question. The
smirk that started just before the screen went black. The alert about the
explosion at Delta Sigma. "I don't know what was on that video, but it was not
Richard. No, " he whispered, " it was not him." Michael's gaze drifted off to
someplace else.

It hit her then, the full, catastrophic weight of it. Stephan. All those years.
The shared sorrow, the quiet support, the scientific legacy she thought they
both honored. All built on a monstrous lie. Her father hadn't succumbed to
stress. He had been murdered by his own creation, and the man she trusted as a
second father had hidden the truth, let her believe a comforting fiction while
he pursued his own hidden agenda. The image of Brown's intense, hopeful gaze as
she sat in the interface chair flashed in her mind. *Perhaps it will respond to
you. As his daughter.* He hadn't just wanted to wake ADAM. He had been looking
for Patches inside it. Using her.

A low sound tore from Edith's throat, a sound of utter betrayal. The impassive,
logical walls she had built around herself crumbled into dust.

Michael saw her shatter, saw the unbearable weight land on her, and his own
fragile control broke completely. A gut-wrenching sob escaped him, then
another, tearing through the quiet room. He doubled over, lowering his head
into his hands, the full horror of Jennifer's death, of Richards's fate, of his
own failures crashing down on him in a tidal wave of guilt and despair and
helpless rage.

Edith didn't know what to do. Her own world was in ruins, her past a lie, her
future terrifyingly uncertain. But seeing Michael utterly broken, stripped bare
of his soldier's composure, reduced to primal, shuddering anguish, something
shifted within her. Some instinct, maternal perhaps, or simply the bond forged
in shared crisis, took over. She reached out tentatively, then pulled him
upright, held him close as he wept, rocking him gently like a child woken from
a nightmare. She held onto him, the two of them weeping, broken souls clinging
together in the dark, cold farmhouse, the silence outside pressing in, vast and
indifferent.
