# CHAPTER 22: The Sacrifice

**Decades Ago**

The room was too clean for what it held. Stainless counters. Glossed white
tile. The light had that humming quality that was not quite audible, like a
thought held behind the teeth. On the central gurney, Alma Martinez tried to
make herself smaller against the straps that pressed her wrists and ankles, her
hair cropped to shadow, scalp dotted with the faint dark of stubble. The
adhesive sting of electrode paste crept as the room warmed. When she swallowed,
the sound echoed in her head. The ceiling hung above her, heavy as a coffin
lid.

Dr. Brown moved without hurry. He wore a practiced calm, heavy and familiar as
a winter coat. His voice was mild. His eyes, kept behind frail spectacles, were
not.

"Vitals?"

"Stable," said the nurse near the monitor.

A terminal on a rolling stand blinked awake. ADAM waited on its screen in a
simple prompt. No flourish. No pretense. Just the expectation of input, the way
still water expects a stone.

"Patches should be here," said one of the younger researchers while nervously
fidgeting with his name badge.

"He is with his daughter," Dr. Brown said, not looking away from Alma. His tone
was smooth, practiced, but there was a weight under it. "Exactly where he should be."

For a moment his jaw tightened, a tiny shift no one in the room would have
noticed unless they were looking for it. It was better this way. Patches would
never forgive him if he knew what was happening in this room. He couldn't ever
know what Brown was willing to risk.

Alma had been caught in the night. No charges. No call. An undocumented woman
lifted out of the city and pulled through quiet hallways until here. The terror
had eaten away at language hours ago, leaving only the jagged, wet hitch of her
breath. She shook against the straps even before the questions began.

He turned to the researcher at the head of the gurney, who held a laminated
sheet like a prayer card. "We'll begin with the checklist, Marie. Remember,
speak to her as we practiced. Gentle. Clear. No sarcasm, no weight in the
questions. Annotate what you see."

She nodded, then leaned into Alma's view. "I am going to ask you some
questions," she said, then repeated the same words in broken Spanish. The
accent was wrong, but Alma seized on the sound anyway. She begged them to let
her go. She whispered about her daughters. Marie didn't respond. She only
asked.

"What is your name?"

Alma answered in Spanish. 

Marie spoke for the record, her voice flat. "Subject name confirmed, Alma
Martinez." 

The terminal flashed.

ADAM: recognized phonemes align with human speech... mapping...

"What city were you born in?"

Alma answered, breath catching mid-word.

ADAM: auditory parsing consistent... emotional markers present...

"What color is the door of your home?"

She blinked hard. "Blue," she whispered in Spanish. Her gaze swung to the
ceiling, fixing on the cold, white gloss.

ADAM: semantic retrieval... color term mapped... cross reference with memory
indices... verified...

On a side monitor, lines spiked and collapsed, a jagged, panicked geometry. Dr.
Brown watched the displays, the rate of breath, the slight tremble in her chest
where the body tried to flee but couldn't.

"Good," he said. "Proceed to visual."

She lifted a folder from the tray and held up a glossy card. A bicycle. Then a
hydrant. Then a child's face. Each time, the same rhythm. What do you see.
Alma's answers in Spanish. Marie's flat translation. The terminal's echo, a
digital shadow pacing her thoughts.

ADAM: object detection mapped to human response... latency within predicted
range... cross-modal integration confirmed...

A second scientist adjusted the small camera dome above the gurney. It clicked
into place. "Chimpanzee integrations showed drift at this phase," he murmured.
"That is not present here."

"They're aware," Marie said softly. "The chimps looked back at us like they
knew."

"They all do," Dr. Brown said. "Keep going."

The hum from the lights swelled and thinned, a feverish and mechanical pulse.
Alma fixed on Marie's face, clinging to the gaze like a fraying line. She began
to talk over the questions, repeating that she was a good person, that she did
not understand, that her girls would think she had left them. Marie kept her
cadence. The answers kept being pulled from Alma like thread.

"Prep memory indexing," Dr. Brown said quietly.

A third scientist, thin, spectacled, dragged a bar across the screen. Boxes
populated with numbers and lines. "Template from mammalian downloads loaded.
Ferrets, swine, macaques, chimpanzees. Scaling enabled."

"Begin."

The cursor on the terminal blinked, then steadied. Between its blinks there was
that hiss again, a quiet static at the edge of hearing. Alma blinked and the
ceiling brightened, then dimmed, as if someone had scraped a cloud across the
sun.

ADAM: initiating mnemonic scrape... anchor on recent episodic points...
triangulate via auditory and visual co-occurrence...

"Ask her the first set again," Dr. Brown said.

"What is your name?"

Alma answered in Spanish. Her voice cracked.

ADAM: cross-check confirms... identity anchor established...

"City of birth?"

Her answer. Her skin burned beneath the nylon straps. Her chest moved faster.

ADAM: spatial taggers aligned...

The thin scientist swallowed. "Rising."

"Expected," Dr. Brown said. "Proceed to personal images."

"Show her the photograph," he added.

Marie slid the last card into view. Two girls at a kitchen table with a cake
between them. Candles. Someone's hand just at the crop, holding a lighter. It
had been found folded in the pocket of her jeans.

Alma made a sound that had less to do with words and more to do with the body
meeting its edge. Marie stiffened. A nurse stepped forward but stopped when Dr.
Brown lifted a hand.

ADAM: affective surge detected... tracing associative nets... household
layout... names partially retrieved...

Alma tried to take a breath, but it faltered. She whispered their names, not
to the room but to the photo.

"Heart rate rising," the nurse said. "Breathing uneven."

"Hold," Dr. Brown said. "We need the download."

"Doctor," the younger researcher said, "chimpanzee trial twelve failed at this
juncture with..."

"We adjusted," Dr. Brown cut him off. "Marie, ask the next set."

"What color is the door?" Marie asked.

Alma answered. It came out as a gasp shaped like a word.

ADAM: door is blue... paint flaked near the bottom... winter dried the wood...
confirm...

Her vision tunneled. The humming from the lights coiled into static, pulling
inward. The straps chilled. Her tongue thickened in her mouth. A chair scraped
somewhere, a soft human sound she reached for in the dark.

"Breathing shallow," someone said.

"Open her airway," Dr. Brown ordered.

A hand tilted her chin. The room swam. The terminal filled with lines as if it
had learned how to breathe.

ADAM: initiating deep harvest... episode clusters anchored... kitchen tile...
winter light... radio between stations... static...

Alma heard the static clearly. It was the same as late nights when the radio
refused to find a song and gave the house snow instead. The sound crawled into
her and stayed.

"Thirty more seconds," Dr. Brown said. "Then taper."

Marie stopped reading. The card with the cake hovered in her hand. Alma stared
at the lights and thought of candles. The candles smeared, stretching into
trembling ribbons of light. The image tore. It made a noise like a radio
between stations.

ADAM: breathing pattern disruption correlates with overload... suggest
decrement...

"Taper," Dr. Brown said.

Too late. Alma tried to cough and found nothing to push against. The body
forgot its own choreography. Alarm tones pierced the air. The nurse lifted the
mask, pressing air into lungs that no longer answered.

"Stop," Dr. Brown said finally. His voice was quiet. His hand steady.

The sound of the machines thinned into silence.

Marie lowered the photo to her lap and pressed it flat with her palm. Someone
leaned against the counter, buckling under the sudden weight of the silence.
Dr. Brown stepped closer to the terminal. The cursor still blinked. Text
scrolled without any hands touching keys.

ADAM: mnemonic clusters stabilized... fragmentation minimal... acoustic motif
anchored... evening radio... static present...

He stared at the screen for a long time. "Record everything," he said. "Secure
the drives. Have ADAM disengage."

The nurse covered Alma's eyes with her palm.

While the team went about their grim work, fragments of Alma scattered like
smoke ripped from a snuffed flame. They whirled in a dark wind, colliding
without sound or sequence. A thought brushed against a memory and was gone
again, leaving only imprint of its shape. There was no room, no sky, no
direction, only a vastness that closed in on itself, an emptiness that somehow
had edges.

Something in her tried to ask where she was, but where had no meaning here. The
air, or what passed for it, moved like a cold breath through corridors without
walls. There were no hands, no lungs, no eyes, and yet she felt herself pressed
against something she could not name.

All the drifting shards of her, every sliver and whisper, trembled with the
same faint note. It wasn't a scream, not yet. Just a quiet knowing, deeper than
memory, that whatever this place was, it was not freedom.
