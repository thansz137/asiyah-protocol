# CHAPTER 2: The Assignment

Michael strode into the cabin. A master sergeant stood at attention, hand
raised in salute.

"At ease, Sergeant." Michael returned the salute while continuing his way down
the aisle. 

A lone woman sat at the main table, her focus absolute on an electronic
notebook. Red hair, pulled back. She wore civilian clothes, but the Sergeant
was giving her a wide berth, standing rigid near the cockpit as if afraid to
disturb her air.

She offered no reaction when Michael entered. As he approached, her head
snapped up. She assessed him in a single sweep, head to boots. The smile she
gave him was brief, not quite reaching her eyes. She rose and extended a hand.

"Lieutenant Michael Grant. Pleasure to meet you."

"Jennifer Smith, NSA."

Her handshake was firm and brief.

"Good to meet you, Jennifer."

"Please," she said, gesturing to the plush leather chair opposite her, "have a
seat and we'll get started."

Michael eased himself down into the deep, cushioned leather of the chair. He
recalled his last flight home from Iraq, a hulking air transport packed with
troops like sardines in a tin. The hot, spartan interior favored utility over
comfort—over *any* comfort. The memory lay in stark contrast to his current
surroundings.

Jennifer didn't wait for him to get comfortable. "I'm going to brief you on the
information I have available right now, but things can change at any moment and
there isn't a lot to go on." She passed him a thick manila folder. "We are
flying to Plainfield, Illinois, a city approximately forty miles southwest of
Chicago. Once there, we will make contact with a civilian named Todd Miles. We
are to convince Mr. Miles to accompany us back to Washington, where he will
meet with the president. I will be the primary contact; your role is strictly
as observer."

"Is Mr. Miles expecting us?" he asked.

"Not to my knowledge."

"You're sending a handler and an analyst to do a pickup," Michael said, looking
at the folder. "If this was a standard recruitment, you'd have called. You
think he's going to run."

"I know, it's unusual as hell, I'm just telling you what we are ordered to do."

Frustrated, Michael flipped through the pages in the folder.

Jennifer leaned forward. "That last part about me talking and you watching is 
important, Mike. Understand?"

Her talking? Yes, that part he understood. She was the NSA specialist. He was
the 'cognitive outlier' they'd pulled from a desk. An asset, not a partner. The
handler and the handled.

"Yes. You talk, I observe. What exactly should I be observing?"

"That's what I need you to help figure out," she replied with a brief, measured
smile. "I know General Arnold wanted you there, watching. The brief does not
say for what specifically. Which tells me he expects you'll know it when you
see it."

"Very well," he said, trying to relax, "what's all this?" He raised the folder
of papers.

"It's our current dossier on Mr. Miles. I have an electronic copy here." She 
raised the thin notebook. "It would be best for both of us to learn as much as 
possible about our contact."

"Will do. Before we start studying though, can I ask why the sergeant is 
here?" Michael nodded to the front of the plane where the soldier had taken a 
seat.

"That's Sergeant Reyes. He has a direct order from the president to ensure the
safety of Mr. Miles on the return flight in case of an in-air emergency. It's
been made clear that Mr. Miles is the priority if anything goes wrong."

Why would the president issue a direct order to a lowly sergeant? The answer
formed before Michael wanted to acknowledge it. He forced himself to examine
the facts anyway.

A direct order could not be countermanded, and left little room for
interference. But who could interfere? Those ranked between Reyes and the
president. *The president doesn't trust the chain of command.*

Michael tucked the unsettling idea away for later evaluation, when he had time
to mull it over. "And if Mr. Miles does not comply and decides to stay in
Plainfield?"

"We can only request that he come with us; we can't force him. If he refuses,
then we regroup and wait for new orders."

"Anything else?"

"That's it. It would be best if we both studied up on Mr. Miles." Jennifer
lightly touched his knee. "I know this is frustrating Mike, I really do. We're
in the same boat here; I hope we can work together on this."

Michael met her gaze. *She's good. And right.* *No point in blaming the
messenger.* "Of course. We'll get through this." Michael smiled as he opened
the dossier, ignoring the drop in his gut as the jet took off.

A puffy face looked up at him from a Driver's License photo; the man's hairline
in full retreat. The next several pages contained nothing but cold facts:
forty-two years old, married with two daughters, a college-educated CPA.
Nothing reached out to grab Michael's attention.

The next section was a digital vivisection of the Miles family. It didn't just
list websites visited; it correlated the millisecond latency in Todd's
keystrokes against his smart-watch biometrics to model his decision-making
tree. A sidebar graph predicted, with 99.8% confidence, exactly which route
Todd would drive home and what brand of milk he would buy when he stopped for
gas. Michael felt a cold prickle of sweat. This wasn't just data aggregation;
it was mathematical determinism. The machine knew Todd better than Todd knew
himself. And looking at the timestamp, this entire profile was complex enough
to take a team of psychologists weeks to compile, yet it had been generated in
under four minutes.

The report continued, breaking Todd down into raw metrics. Introvert. Craves
stability. Strong numerical talent. Politically liberal. Fiscally conservative.
The list went on and on, reducing a human life to a dataset. According to the
algorithm, Todd Miles was a ghost, a man with zero statistical deviation from
the norm. Following each heading was a detailed section composed of arcane
technical terms, only some of which Michael understood.

He let the information stew in his mind as he looked out the window, watching 
thin wisps of clouds pass by. A word, a single word, came bubbling up to the 
edge of his awareness. *Boring.*

Todd's life was a boring plod down the middle path of mediocrity. Or, as 
Michael chewed on the thought, it was the perfect cover.

He looked across at Jennifer. "So, how do you plan on approaching Mr. 
Miles?"

She met his gaze, welcoming the break. "That's a great question, Mike. They
haven't given me much to work with here."

"Have they given any reasons why the president wants to meet with him?"

"No, no they haven't." She leaned back slightly, eyes steady on his. "And that
means we adapt. I'll make the introduction, extend the invitation. When he asks
questions I can't answer, I'll tell him that directly. Sometimes honesty is the
best approach when you can't offer details." She tapped the notebook once,
decisive. "I've done extractions with less to work with. The key is reading the
person in front of you and adjusting in real time."

He leaned forward. "Let me ask you something, and I understand if you can't
answer it. Have you ever been involved in a mission like this before?"

"No. Never." Her tone was final.

"Neither have I," Michael admitted. He dropped the formal military bearing,
offering her a look of genuine, unguarded optimism. "But look on the bright
side. At least we're in this together."

Jennifer looked at him, seemingly surprised by his lack of ego. The stiff,
professional mask slipped, replaced by a soft smile that finally reached her
eyes. She held his gaze for a beat longer than necessary before turning back to
her work.
