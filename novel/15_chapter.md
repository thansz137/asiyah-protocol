# CHAPTER 15: L'Absence

**FOUR DAYS EARLIER - Paris, France**

Prime Minister Marie Jacotin anchored the head of the mahogany table. Seventy
years had refined her silhouette rather than eroded it. She wore severe black,
a sharp contrast to the chaotic spread of dossiers before her. Her hair was a
tight silver coil, her nails blood-red against the white papers. She didn't
just lead the conference; she commanded the silence between the arguments.

Down the length of the table, the monolithic bloc of dignitaries fractured into
casual clusters. Portfolios snapped shut. Shoulders dropped. The air in the
room shifted from the stale tension of trade tariffs to the warm anticipation
of long lunches and vintage Bordeaux.

Marie kept her attention fixed on the Trade Minister seated to her right,
nodding as he outlined the final concessions. But as he spoke, her focus began
to slide. Her eyes drifted from his face to the empty air just above his
shoulder. She reclined slowly, easing her spine against the leather. She
settled into the chair, her body relaxing while her eyes widened, fixing on a
distant point with absolute, silent clarity.

The Trade Minister faltered mid-sentence. He exchanged a sharp look with the
official on Marie's left, and they rose in unison. The Minister touched her
shoulder, whispering her name. She remained stone. The ambient chatter withered
as heads turned toward the sudden silence at the head of the table. Someone
shouted for the hallway detail. The double doors flew open, and security agents
flooded the room.

Her eyelids fluttered, then sealed shut. Her posture, already settled,
surrendered completely to gravity. She listed sideways, becoming dead weight as
the detail team scrambled to catch her before she hit the carpet. The room
erupted into chaos. The lead agent keyed his wrist mic. "Medical emergency.
Primary is down. Notify SAMU. We need a transport at the south entrance. Now."

The SAMU team swept in, cutting through the panic. They worked in silence.
Pupils checked. Airway secured. Body transferred. Philippe, her aide, refused
to be separated. He shouldered past a security officer to climb into the back
of the transport. Opposite him, a medic anchored a line into Marie's forearm
with clinical precision as the doors slammed shut.

The siren tore through the canyon of old streets, the blue strobes washing over
the cabin interior. Philippe leaned over the stretcher, demanding answers the
medic couldn't give. Marie gasped. A sharp, ugly sound. Her eyes snapped open,
wild and searching, struggling to process the swaying ceiling. The medic began
his soothing script, but she ignored him. She found Philippe. Her hand lashed
out, seizing his wrist. Her fingers dug into his skin, desperate and trembling.

"Philippe." Her voice was a dry rasp. "Connect me to Albert Laurent."

Philippe hesitated, glancing at the medic. "The Director of CNES? Madame, you
need to rest."

Marie's panic vanished, replaced by the cold iron of statecraft. She pulled
Philippe down until their faces were inches apart.

"I do not need rest. I need the Space Director. Get him on the line."

Philippe placed a steadying hand on her shoulder, his voice dropping to a
soothing, patronizing murmur. "Madame, please. You have had a severe shock. We
are minutes from the hospital. Just lie back."

"Now."
