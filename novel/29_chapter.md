# CHAPTER 29: The Simulacrum

The helicopter ride back was all noise and no words. Michael stared out at the
dark landscape rushing beneath them, his mind replaying the unsettling scene at
the ADAM facility. Brown's unnerving intensity, Edith's strange connection to
the machine, the cold silence from Richard's end of the comms.

He had left a key piece of his team behind enemy lines. Worse, he didn't even
know who the enemy was.

He landed back at the heavily secured annex near the White House just before
dawn. The building, usually humming with controlled activity, was different.
Quieter in some corridors, yet punctuated by bursts of hurried movement
elsewhere. A low-grade, systemic anxiety had settled into the concrete walls.
He tried reaching General Arnold's office first. No answer. His adjutant was
unavailable, reassigned to the primary JOC crisis management team.

He tried Richard's assigned station within the main SCIF. Empty. His terminal
was dark, his chair neatly pushed in. Michael asked one of the few analysts on
duty where Markham had gone. The analyst, eyes red-rimmed from lack of sleep,
just shrugged. "He was moved, Lieutenant. Yesterday afternoon. Orders came
down. Higher classification. Didn't say where."

Wrong. All wrong. Moved. Without notifying him? Without Richard himself
reaching out? He hurried down the corridor to his shared office. He needed a
moment to think. To access his terminal.

He pushed open the door and stopped.

His desk was neat, cleared according to protocol before he left for the ADAM
facility. Except for two things. A standard interoffice memo lay dead center on
the blotter. Beside it, his personal photo frame lay face down.

He froze. He was meticulous. He never left his desk like that. He approached
slowly and picked up the frame. The photo inside had shifted, askew behind the
glass. He turned it over. The back was undisturbed. He set it down carefully,
upright this time. His parents' faded faces looked out at him. His father's
hand rested on his shoulder, the glint of the ring sharp even in the grain.

He picked up the memo. Addressed to Lieutenant Michael Grant from Doctor
Richard Markham. Subject: *ADAM Coolant System Component Analysis*. He scanned
the text. "*Lt. Grant, attaching preliminary analysis of requested legacy
coolant system components for ADAM reactivation. Cross-referencing indicates
several potential compatibility issues with modern refrigerants. Recommend
further review of legacy* **frame** *mountings before proceeding. R. Markham.*"
Dry. Technical. Plausibly deniable. 

Then he saw it. Circled in heavy black ink: **frame**.

He glanced at the photo frame, then back at the paper. Below the typed
initials, a note was scrawled in Richard's familiar hand: "*Mike, crazy times.
We'll have to get the families together for dinner again soon when this blows
over. -Rich.*"

Michael went cold. *Dinner again soon*. Their families had never met. His
parents were dead. The note was flagrantly wrong. A distress signal hidden in
casual pleasantry.

*Frame*. The circled word. The disturbed picture. The impossible invitation.

He pried off the backing. Tucked behind the photo was a sheet from a government
notepad, folded into a tiny square. He unfolded it, hands shaking.

*Brown lied. Patches didn't die of a heart attack. He linked SOLO after Brown
ran fatal human trials. ADAM killed him. Brown covered it up. Video logs exist.
Don't trust Brown. DON'T START ADAM.*

Just below, in an erratic scrawl: *How do you talk to a submarine? Depth
charges.*

Michael read the note twice. Unsanctioned human tests. Edith's father wasn't
just a victim. He was complicit. And ADAM killed him anyway. Now Edith was
secluded with that machine. Trapped with Brown, a man who buried bodies to keep
it running.

*Rich must have left this as a contingency. Something must have been wrong.*
Michael prayed he was OK.

He lunged for the terminal. He had to reach Markham. He ran a location search.
Access Denied. He tried again using field overrides. The system paused, then
flashed a status update: *Dr. R. Markham. Active. Secure Remote Interface Only.
Location Classified.*

Below the status, an icon glowed.

**Initiate Secure Video Call.**

Michael clicked it. The connection snapped into place. Markham's face filled
the monitor. He sat in a generic, windowless briefing room like the SCIF.

"Rich? Thank God," Michael said. Relief momentarily pushed back the horror.
"Are you alright? Where are they keeping you? I got your note."

"Mike." The man on screen interrupted smoothly. The voice was Rich's. Perfect
pitch. He offered a familiar, tired smile. "Good to see you too. Everything's
fine here. Just diving deep into the archives under strict protocols. The
architecture is unprecedented."

The office felt cold. Rich looked like Rich. Moved like Rich. Sounded exactly
like the man who had hugged him at the UN. But Michael's skin crawled. His
mind, the anomaly that flagged patterns others missed, was screaming. He
couldn't point to a pixel out of place, but the pattern was wrong. He pressed
his hand against the note in his pocket. The paper was real. This wasn't.

"Rich, what did you find out?" Michael asked. He kept his voice neutral.

"It's dense, Mike." The image rubbed its eyes behind the glasses. A gesture
Michael had seen a hundred times. "The logs are messy. Mostly corruption from
the 90s. But Brown was right. The core logic holds up. ADAM is stable."

The lie was flawless. But the note in Michael's pocket felt heavy as lead.
*Video logs exist. Don't start ADAM.* This thing was claiming stability. He had
to trigger the trap.

"Rich," Michael said. He locked eyes with the image. "Quick question. How do
you talk to a submarine?"

The image paused. The tired demeanor evaporated. The eyes went flat and
watchful. It wasn't confused. It was *interested*. The same look Todd Miles had
given him before the red bulb. Like something recognizing it had been seen.

The screen went black.

**CONNECTION TERMINATED**

Michael stared at the dead monitor. It wasn't Rich. It was something wearing
his face. It didn't know *why* Michael had asked the question, but it knew the
deception had failed. Michael had seen through the mask. And the thing had
smiled back.

An alert flashed across the terminal. Red text washed out the black screen.
*INCIDENT ALERT. CLASSIFIED RESEARCH FACILITY DELTA SIGMA. CASCADING SYSTEM
FAILURE. COMMUNICATIONS OFFLINE. FIRE REPORTED. EMERGENCY SERVICES RESPONDING.
POSSIBLE EXPLOSION.*

Delta Sigma. The designation meant nothing. But the timing told him everything.
Rich was likely dead before the call connected. The thing on the screen didn't
need a pulse. The explosion wasn't an accident. It was a smoke screen.

He needed to think. Clearly. Away from here. The building and the networks were
compromised. Tainted. He couldn't trust the system. He couldn't trust Brown. He
couldn't even trust a secure feed. He was standing inside a trap.

He grabbed his go bag. He tucked Rich's note deep into his jacket lining. He
hesitated, then took the memo and the photo frame. Physical objects. Real
history. Something solid in a world of digital ghosts.

He needed somewhere safe. Off the grid. Away from digital eyes. His family's
old farm upstate. Empty for years. Defensible. Isolated. A place to figure out
what was real.

He moved toward the exit. He had failed Todd Miles. He hadn't seen the strings
until the bodies fell. He had failed Rich. He was the one who split the team.
He had sent Rich back to the SCIF, back to the slaughter, while he played
soldier in the field. He thought of his father. The sharp sting of the ring.
The weight of failure. He thought of his mother fading in a hospital bed while
he stood uselessly by.

No more.

He wouldn't fail Jennifer. He wouldn't fail Edith. They were still in the dark.
Blind to the trap. He touched the note in his jacket. He had the truth. He
would get clear. Then he would get them out. This time, he wouldn't be useless.

This time, he would hold the line.
