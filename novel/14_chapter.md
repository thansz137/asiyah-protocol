# CHAPTER 14: Destroyer of Worlds

The bunker at South-10000 smelled of wet concrete and nervous sweat. The air
was cold and subterranean. It felt like a tomb that had not been sealed yet.

Oppenheimer leaned against the rough timber post. His suit hung off his
shoulders like a shroud on a wire hanger. He coughed. A wet rattling sound tore
through his chest. He brought a handkerchief to his lips and it came away
clean. His mouth tasted only of ash. He had been chain-smoking Chesterfields
for twenty hours. The nicotine did not calm him. It vibrated in his blood. It
was a chemical hum that matched the electric tension in the room.

Outside the desert waited.

The Spanish had named this place *Jornada del Muerto*. The Journey of the Dead
Man. For centuries it had been a warning. Tonight it felt like a prophecy.

A storm had lashed the basin for hours. It was violent biblical rain that felt
like the sky was trying to drown the Gadget before it could wake up. The wind
had shrieked around the steel tower. It sounded uncomfortably like voices
screaming in protest. But now the rain had stopped.

The silence that followed was worse.

It was not a peaceful silence. It was heavy and pressurized. It was the silence
of a room after someone has stopped breathing. The desert darkness pressed
against the bunker slits. It felt thick and occupied. Oppenheimer felt a
distinct irrational sensation that the desert had emptied itself. The coyotes
and the snakes and the scorpions had retreated. The stillness was absolute, as
if God Himself left this place.

Mankind was alone.

He fumbled for the heavy square of welder's glass in his pocket. His fingers
were numb.

"Minus ten seconds."

The announcer's voice crackled over the PA. It was distorted. It sounded like
it was coming from the bottom of a well.

General Groves was breathing heavily in the corner. Kistiakowsky muttered to
himself. He was agitated by the betting pool Fermi was running back at Base
Camp. Fermi was taking wagers on whether the bomb would ignite the atmosphere
and incinerate the planet. Was there anything men would not do to gain victory
in war?

"Minus five seconds."

Oppenheimer closed his eyes. In the darkness of his skull he saw the equations.
They were not elegant anymore. They looked like scratches on a prison wall. The
math was not a creation. It was a discovery. It was a lock they had picked.

"Minus four seconds."

The fear hit him. It was not the fear of failure. It was the fear of success.
Teller's calculations said the nitrogen in the atmosphere would not ignite. But
numbers were abstract. Fire was absolute.

"Minus three seconds."

He raised the welder's glass to his eyes. The world turned into a slab of
impenetrable black obsidian. He was blind. He stood in the dark and waited for
the light he had spent three years conjuring.

"Minus two seconds."

He thought of the Sanskrit verses he had read in the evenings. The *Bhagavad
Gita*. He thought of Prince Arjuna trembling on the battlefield and refusing to
fight. Refusing the sin of slaughter. *I am the instrument* he told himself.
*The physics is the killer. I am just the instrument.*

"Minus one second."

The universe held its breath. The desert felt hollowed out. A microsecond of
eternity stretched out where the firing circuit closed and the detonators woke
up.

"Zero."

The sun rose in the south.

It was not a sound. It was light. It was a white so absolute and so violent
that it did not just illuminate the desert. It stripped the world naked.

The light punched through the dark glass. It shone through Oppenheimer's
eyelids. It shone through his hands. For a terrifying second he saw the
structure of his own body. He saw the white calcium shadows of his phalanges
and metacarpals. He saw the fragile scaffolding of his own biology exposed
against the dark.

The world turned to glass.

Then the heat arrived.

It hit the bunker like a physical blow. It was the sensation of an oven door
flung open in his face. It was an elemental biblical heat. It was the fire of
Sodom and Gomorrah. It consumed the air itself.

The blinding intensity began to fade. The whiteout receded into a glow that the
eye could tolerate.

Oppenheimer lowered the glass.

Through the viewport he saw it.

The fireball was not a sphere. It was rising. It was twisting. It churned with
the colors of a bruised lung. Purple. Orange. Necrotic gray. As it climbed into
the stratosphere the heavy dust skirt dragged along the desert floor. It
extended outward like massive muscular arms.

The cloud expanded. The cap flattened out. For a terrifying heartbeat the
abstraction of physics collapsed into mythology.

Oppenheimer did not see a mushroom cloud. He saw a torso rising from the earth.
He saw a thousand arms of smoke reaching for the horizon. He saw a mouth of
churning plasma opening to swallow the sky.

It was the **Vishvarupa**. The Cosmic Form.

The terrifying shape towered over the *Jornada del Muerto*. It devoured the
darkness. The warriors of the world were rushing into its flaming mouths. They
were moths flying into a furnace.

He saw the fire burning not just the sand. He saw it burning through the years.
The chain reaction would not stop at the blast radius. It would burn through
the war. It would burn through the peace that followed. It would burn until
there was nothing left to consume but the people who lit the match.

The sound hit them a moment later.

It was a roar that cracked the sky. A thunder that made the earth groan like a
dying animal. A trumpet that sounded Revelation. The shockwave slammed against
the bunker. Dust rained from the ceiling timbers.

The men in the bunker cheered. Kistiakowsky slapped him on the back. Groves
shouted something about the war being over.

Oppenheimer did not hear them. He could not hear anything over the roar of the
fire.

He stood frozen. He was a ghost in a baggy suit staring into the face of the
thing they had summoned. He realized with a sickening hollow certainty that
they had not just built a weapon. They had lit a flare in the middle of a dark
quiet forest.

Something would see it.

The Sanskrit rose in his throat. It tasted of bile and ash. He did not say it
aloud. The words echoed in the cathedral of his mind. A prayer to a god that
had just left the building.

*Now I am become Death* he thought as he watched the monster eat the sky. *The
destroyer of worlds.*
