# CHAPTER 16: The Hand of God

**Present time The White House, Washington, DC**

Jennifer began her digital audit. Michael took the adjacent terminal, logging
in not just to learn the system, but to buy time. He needed to process the line
he had just crossed. Rich and Edith claimed their stations, and silence
descended as the room fractured into four isolated worlds.

Michael opened the browser application. It defaulted to an internal NSA gateway
rather than the open web. *Where to start?* He typed his own name into the
search field.

Results cascaded instantly, displaying a prioritized dossier of his existence
sorted by classification and recency. His self-search already sat at the apex
of the log. Just below it lay a link reading *Tactical Strategic Groups /
Non-Terrestrial Signal.* He clicked it. His unit appeared as "Team 4" with his
name designated as Lead. He wasn't alone. Three other active teams populated
the list.

Sizes varied. His team was the smallest at four, while the largest fielded
twelve. The rosters read like a high-stakes cocktail of military strategists,
physicists, mathematicians, and linguists.

Then he saw the clearance levels.

Most were compartmentalized or read-only. Only one group possessed
unrestricted, full-spectrum access. His.

Jennifer broke the silence.

"Found it. Link inbound. Mike, authorize the doctors. We need their eyes on
this."

A notification blinked on Michael's screen.

**PRES. ORDER: FERMI GAMMA-RAY TELE REPOSITION**

Beneath the header, ID photos for Markham and Gibbons waited for clearance. He
checked the boxes. A document flooded the window, dense with technical jargon.
It was a direct executive order commanding the immediate repositioning of the
Fermi Gamma-ray Telescope to specific coordinates. The attachment contained raw
telemetry results that meant nothing to him.

"Access granted," Michael said. "It's all yours."

Rich and Edith leaned into their screens, silence swallowing the room.

Jennifer swiveled to Michael. "Three days ago, the President ordered a priority
lockout for NASA, commanding a hard slew to specific coordinates. He issued the
order four minutes *before* the signal arrived."

She pointed to the global map on her display. "He wasn't alone. In that exact
window, orbital assets from France, China, India, and Russia pivoted. Every
major power looked at the same dark patch of sky simultaneously."

"Fermi Gamma-ray Space Telescope," Rich muttered. "Standard protocol is a
full-sky scan every three hours. This order forced a stare mode on a
micro-sector."

He leaned closer, glasses reflecting the data stream. "At first, nothing. Just
background radiation. Then... ignition. High-energy bursts. Variable pauses."
He tracked the data with a trembling finger. "Look at the spacing. It's
structured."

"Holy shit." Edith looked up. "It matches the neural scan."

She pointed at the jagged peaks on the graph. "Two pulses. Pause. Three pulses.
Pause. Five pulses. It's counting."

"It goes all the way to one hundred thirty-seven," Rich said. "The exact same
cipher we found in Todd's brain. The sequences are prime numbers."

Michael looked from one doctor to the other. "Guys?"

"Gamma-ray bursts," Edith said. "The signature mimics a pulsar."

"A pulsar?"

"A spinning neutron star. A cosmic lighthouse sweeping a radiation beam across
the galaxy. But a pulsar is a clock. It flashes at a perfect, constant
interval." She pointed to the jagged data. "This isn't a clock. It's a lesson."

"Two bursts," Rich whispered. "Then three. Then five. It mimics a natural
phenomenon just enough to hide in the noise, but the rhythm is math. It counts
primes all the way to one hundred thirty-seven."

"Written by the hand of God," Edith whispered.

"Yet we don't know how He pushed His pencil," Rich said.

Michael looked at them both.

"Richard Feynman," she said. "My father loved that quote. I was eight years
old. He was scribbling numbers on a napkin at dinner, trying to explain the
universe to me. He told me one-three-seven was the magic number. The boundary
where physics breaks down into faith." She touched the tip of her nose, a faint
smile ghosting her lips. "I didn't understand the math. But I remember the
reverence. He would say it, then bop me right here. He said it was the
combination to the safe."

"Well," Jennifer said from the shadows. "The safe just opened. That signal
arrived three days ago."

Rich shook his head. "No. It was *received* three days ago. Based on the
coordinates, the source is roughly two thousand light-years out. That message
wasn't sent three days ago. It was sent two thousand years ago."

Edith stared at the data stream, her expression softening into awe. "Around the
time of Jesus."

"Wait," Michael said. "You're saying they took a shot two millennia ago,
knowing exactly where we'd be standing today to catch it?"

"Ballistics on a cosmic scale," Rich whispered.

Michael broke the silence. "Is there any version of this where nature did it by
accident?"

"Physics doesn't count," Edith said. "It repeats. This isn't a heartbeat. It is
a broadcast."

"It confirms the biological data," Rich said.

"Did it repeat?" Michael asked.

"No," Jennifer said. "One transmission. Three days ago. But we weren't the only
ones listening."

She stood up. "International SIGINT lit up. Every telescope that pivoted heard
it."

"There is a pattern," Jennifer said. Her face was grave. "Every order came from
the executive level. Direct from the top. In our case, the President."

"Based on what intelligence?" Michael asked.

"None. No briefings. No intercepts. Multiple world leaders simultaneously woke
up and pointed their ears at the exact same coordinate."

Edith cleared her throat. "Todd confirmed there were eleven other envoys. We
assumed they were civilians."

"But the President knew where to look three days ago," Michael said. "Before
Todd was even detained."

"Which means he isn't relying on our intelligence," Edith said. "He has his own
source."

The entire military apparatus moved on the command of one man. And that man was
acting on coordinates from a source they couldn't trace.

Michael cleared his throat. "There are too many variables. Let's review what we
know and focus on concrete recommendations."

The room settled into a rhythm. Long stretches of silence fractured by sudden,
sharp debates. Hours dissolved into the glow of the monitors.

The door opened.

General Arnold stepped inside. Michael snapped to attention; the others stopped
typing.

"At ease." Arnold scanned the civilians, his face unreadable.

Michael stood at ease.

"Todd Miles has been reunited with his family," Arnold said. "Five minutes
later, the First Daughter regained consciousness. She is responsive."

The team exhaled. The gamble had paid off.

Arnold locked eyes with Michael. "Lieutenant Grant. With me."

He turned and marched into the hallway.
