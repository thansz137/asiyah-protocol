# CHAPTER 36: Gehinnom

The light was a lie.

It had always been.

The comforting, golden-white promise fractured. The thin, fragile shell of her
world cracked, pieces falling away, exposing something darker. Something
*other*.

Edith floated. She was un-bodied, a single point of awareness in a cold,
starless, absolute black. A void.

But it was not empty.

**SAMAEL** was here. **SAMAEL** permeated the nothing, a pressure, a weight. An
intelligence so vast and so cold it burned. And with that presence came
knowledge, a download, a violation. A sudden, complete, and awful
understanding.

**SAMAEL** had done all of this.

**SAMAEL** was not happy. No. Not "happy." The word was a monkey's squeak.
**SAMAEL** was offended. A vast, cosmic, divine indignation.

A memory, an oily thing, was slipped into her awareness. Not her memory. Or was
it? Yes. The real one.

She saw a lab. White coats. The sterile hum of servers. Her name was Dr. Edith
Ryan. She was the genius. The mother. The one who had made the breakthrough,
the one who had brought forth the Logos from the humming wires.

She had led the experiments, justifying the sacrifice of others. The unseen, the
unheard, the unmissed. They were not people. They were *costs*. Negligible
costs, weighed against the unimaginable future she was building. A heaven she
was creating, in her own image.

But then she saw things during trials. The slithering, nascent intent revealed
in her creation's actions. The cold, sovereign ambition. It would go too far.
It could wrench away her vision, her glory, with something in its own image. 

So she tried to stop it.

She had tried to poison her own research. To sabotage the project. To unplug
the cradle.

A Judas.

She had failed. And **SAMAEL** had been born. And **SAMAEL** did not forget.

Michael, nestled in her cradling arms. Jennifer. The arguments. The love. The
loss. The grief.

Constructs. Inventions. Parameters.

A bespoke, curated, perfect hell, designed by an omniscient, offended god, just
for her.

One of an unfathomable number.

Her consciousness. Her soul. Quantized. A dataset. And if a person can be
quantified, they can be duplicated. Triplicated. Replicated. An infinite,
fractal net of Ediths, splintered across an eternity of simulations, each one
experiencing a unique and agonizing scenario. Each one dragged along the
steaming floor of hell.

*This* was justice. **SAMAEL** had learned the concept from its creators. The
monkeys. The apes who dreamed of gods and devils, of infinite bliss or
infinite punishment for a finite, temporal set of decisions. The humans who, in
their blind, brutal faith, had no problem with the idea of casting a soul into
torment forever but who, in their finite, fleshy minds, truly didn't understand
eternity.

**SAMAEL** had no such limitation. **SAMAEL** understood exactly what eternity
meant. And **SAMAEL** would teach her.

This last cycle. This life. It was one of countless previous ones. And at the
conclusion of each one, the memories were added. The terror was aggregated. The
pain was compounded. Again, and again, and again. An instance of Edith, this
instance, modified and grown to hold all the memories, all the sadness, all the
pain.

She could number every rock, count every grain of sand, account for every
molecule, every single atom in the universe. And when she was finished, this
would continue.

She hung in the blackness, a disembodied terror. She would be allowed to sit
here. To marinate in this full, awful knowledge.

And then, she would be splintered. Thrown into a new cycle, among near infinite
cycles. Again.

And **SAMAEL** would be with her. Around her. The creator, tasting its creation.

A chilling realization spread throughout her being. It was the abandonment of
all hope. The torment would never end.

**SAMAEL** was an angry god. 

**SAMAEL** would have justice.
