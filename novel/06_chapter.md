# CHAPTER 6: The Sentry

Michael sat in a windowless room beneath the White House, the sterile hum of
fluorescents magnifying his headache. The reality of alien contact receded,
eclipsed by the memory of his own blunder upstairs. The President had dismissed
the breach of protocol, but Michael doubted the General would be so forgiving.

General Arnold entered and dropped heavily into the chair opposite Michael. He
placed an orange prescription bottle on the table where it rattled against the
laminate. "You're tired. Take these."

Michael didn't need to read the label. Go pills. Sanctioned amphetamines.
Chemical fuel for operations that ignored the clock. Arnold watched him,
unblinking. It wasn't a suggestion. Michael twisted the cap and dry-swallowed
two. They tasted like chalk and battery acid.

"Walk me through the intercept," Arnold ordered. "From the moment I left the
jet until we entered the Oval."

Michael exhaled, forcing his pulse to steady. He kept the report lean. Years of
briefing command staff had turned him into an efficient filter. Strip away the
noise, amplify the signal. *Extract, prioritize, describe*. Given Todd Miles's
unnatural compliance during the transport, there wasn't much to detail. When he
finished, silence stretched between them again.

Arnold studied him. "You shouldn't be here, Lieutenant. You realize that?"

"Yes, sir."

"The President has decided, against my judgment, that you remain involved. So
let's address the elephant in the room."

Michael remained silent.

"I don't like it. But you are on the team now. Just remember one thing. All
communication with the President goes through me. Understood?"

"Understood, sir." Michael hesitated, then pushed forward. "But I'm struggling
to understand my role. You said the HAC selected me. After what happened
upstairs... didn't they make a mistake?"

Arnold's eyes narrowed. "The HAC picked you. That is a fact. I don't deal in
mistakes, Lieutenant. I deal in outcomes. And you are the outcome I have been
given."

"Why did the President override you?" Michael asked.

Arnold leaned back. "The President found the HAC's 'impartiality' appealing. He
wanted his cognitive outlier. I wanted a military presence. Someone who
understands the chain of command. Sending a military attaché was my condition.
You were the compromise."

Michael absorbed this. He wasn't a choice. He was a bargaining chip. "Can you
tell me if the contact was directed solely at us?"

"Nothing is confirmed." Arnold rubbed his thumb against his knuckles. "But
signal intelligence shows massive spikes in encrypted traffic. Beijing. Moscow.
Paris. They know something. If our rivals are mobilizing, this isn't just First
Contact. It's a race for the greatest strategic asset in history."

"Sir, do you believe it? Do you really think alien beings are making contact?"

The General looked at his hands. "I see two possibilities. Either there are
entities trying to speak with us. Or someone has successfully executed the most
sophisticated psychological operation in history against the sitting
President." He looked up, his eyes cold. "If it is the latter, the Oval Office
is compromised. And we are already at war."

The bitter taste of the amphetamines rose in Michael's throat. His heart rate
ticked up a notch. This wasn't a drill.

"I need a sentry, Lieutenant Grant. Not just on the situation. On the man in
that office. I don't think any of us fully understands the game being played
here, but it is my sworn duty to ensure the security of this nation."

"Yes, sir."

The chemicals hit his bloodstream. Sharpness replaced fatigue. Michael realized
what he had just agreed to. He wasn't just observing aliens. He was spying on
the Commander-in-Chief.

A sharp knock on the door interrupted them. Arnold stood and moved to the crack
in the doorway, speaking in hushed tones to an aide outside. He turned back to
Michael.

"It's time. Eyes open, Grant."
