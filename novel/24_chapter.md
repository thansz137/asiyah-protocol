# CHAPTER 24: Lazarus

The bottle was half-empty on the desk. It had been full an hour ago.

Michael leaned back in the chair, shoulders heavy, the dim desk lamp casting
long shadows across the room. The walls felt closer lately. Everything did.
Since the United Nations assembly, the global order had fractured.
Cyberattacks. Troop movements. Leaders whispering in bunkers. The silence was
brittle. Everyone waiting for the support beams to snap.

He rubbed his temple with the back of his hand and stared at the photograph on
his desk. His family. A cheap print in a chipped frame. His father's hand
rested on his shoulder in the picture, the gold band on his finger catching the
light. He could still feel it now. Not the palm, but the ring. The hand didn't
just strike... it bit. The gold caught the skin above his ear, a hard, clean
edge raking against bone. The sting remained decades later. Not a blow that
crushed, but a scar carved into a child's heart.

His mother's smile in the photo was soft and strained, the kind that tried to
hold the world together without a single word. She had tried to protect him. He
had tried to protect her. Neither had succeeded.

He remembered the silence that followed his father's rage. It filled the house
like smoke. He would stand outside her door, small and shaking, not knowing if
she was crying or already asleep. Years later, the same helplessness returned
in a hospital hallway, where machines clicked softly and her body withered to
cancer. He had stood there again, useless. Always useless.

He pushed away from the desk and turned on the television. He couldn't bear the
news anymore. The faces, the crises, the endless cycle of blame and fear. The
channel landed on a televangelist. A man with teeth capped in porcelain and a
suit tailored for glory, palms turned upward to catch digital grace. He was
selling salvation to a camera lens. Michael ignored the scripture, listening
only to the rhythm. It filled the room like anesthetic gas... dulling the sharp
edges of his mind.

His eyelids grew heavy. The preacher's voice became a slow tide... pulling him
away from the shore of the waking world. The bottle sat untouched. The light
failed. Sleep didn't come gently. It dragged him under.

*The air in the dream was dry, gritty, the kind that left dust on the tongue. He
was small again.*

*The crowd pressed in... a wall of unwashed wool and heat. Faces were smeared
with dust, eyes wide in the pale morning light. Voices swarmed, growing from a
murmur to a low roar. Lazarus. The name passed between them like a secret
currency. Awe mixed with the copper taste of doubt. Michael caught the
fragments drifting through the shifting bodies.*

*"They say he was sick for days."*

*"He is dead. Four days. The smell is already there."*

*"They say the Rabbi is coming."*

*"Then why did he wait?"*

*"They say he has power."*

*"They say many things."*

*Michael pushed forward, the way children move through the world of adults,
slipping between robes and legs. The tomb came into view, a hollow of stone cut
into the earth, its entrance open. The air around it was still. Not quiet.
Still.*

*The crowd surged toward the Rabbi... then recoiled. The smell hit them first.
Heavy and stagnant. A woman wept into her dirty palms. A man spat into the
dust, muttering, "It is too late. The spirit leaves after three days. Today is
the fourth." Another voice cut through the heat, sharp with accusation. "He
could have cured a fever. No one cures death."*

*The Rabbi stood at the mouth of the tomb.*

*He was not glowing or distant. His robe was dusty from the road, his hair
clung to his forehead in the heat. He looked exhausted... but not from the
heat. It was the weariness of a man holding up a collapsing sky. His chest
heaved, eyes locked on the dark void of the tomb. He didn't move. He stood
absolute. He wasn't praying. A bead of sweat rolled into the corner of his eye,
yet he didn't blink. The wailing behind him rose to a shriek, yet he didn't
turn. He stared into the black mouth of the cave, his chest held perfectly
still.*

*The murmurs around him faded. Even the weeping dimmed. The silence held.*

*The Rabbi lifted his head. He didn't shout. He didn't chant. He spoke into the
dark using the same rough tongue as the crowd, yet the words carried a
terrifying weight. They didn't float on the air... they anchored it. "Lazarus."
The name wasn't a request. It was a summons. "Come out." Michael felt the
command vibrate in his own marrow. It felt ancient. Elemental. An absolute,
holy authority that compelled the dust to obey.*

*The man emerged from the tomb.*

*The figure shuffled into the light. The linen strips were tight, binding his
arms to his sides, stained with burial spices and fluids. He didn't walk... he
lurched. It was the jerky, unnatural rhythm of a body remembering how to be a
body. The smell hit Michael instantly. Not just damp earth and flesh. It was
the stale, rank scent of air exhaled from a lung that hadn't breathed in four
days.*

*The Rabbi turned his head.*

*He didn't look at the crowd. He looked at Michael. The stone and heat vanished.
The stillness pressed down, heavy as deep water. It wasn't just eye contact. It
was recognition. Across two thousand years of dust and silence, the figure at
the tomb wasn't looking at a child in a dream. He was looking at a soldier in a
collapsing world.*

*The whispers behind him faded. Michael didn't feel fear. He felt the specific
ache he had carried since the hospital hallway... the desperate need for an
authority absolute enough to reverse the irreversible. The Rabbi's gaze didn't
offer comfort. It offered a promise. The dust, the heat, the tomb... it didn't
dissolve. It shattered into white.*

---

*For Alma Martinez, the world was glass.*

*She was soaring. Weightless. Drifting on an air current that felt, not like
wind, but like a warm, sterile breath from an unseen vent. Below her, the land
was a mosaic of oversaturated pigment. Fields of violet that never swayed.
Rivers of gold flowing in a perfect, repeating loop. Mountains of jade with
edges too sharp for nature.*

*There was no sound. Only a low, subsonic thrum that she felt in her teeth more
than she heard with her ears.*

*She tried to laugh, a bubble of pure, unanchored joy. But the sound came out as
a dry click.*

*She looked down. The jade mountains didn't just flicker... they desynchronized.
For a single, cold frame, the violet fields vanished. In their place was a
wireframe abyss... rigid white geometry suspended in a processing void. Then
the color snapped back.*

*The sterile wind died. The thrum in her teeth sharpened into a high-voltage
hum. Gravity returned... not as a force, but as a cage. She was no longer
flying. She was being held.*
