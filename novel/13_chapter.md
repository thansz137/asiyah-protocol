# CHAPTER 13: The Rubicon

Michael was soon connected to General Arnold on a secure line. He put the 
intercom system on speakerphone, so everyone in the conference room could 
hear the call. They all waited quietly for the general's voice.

"General Arnold speaking," came the abrupt greeting through the intercom.

"General, it's Grant. You're on speaker with the team."

"Report."

"Sir, we have a proposal regarding the First Daughter. But first, what is her
status?"

"She's still in a coma and non-responsive. What's your proposal?"

"Sir, two-part recommendation. First, extract Todd Miles from the White House
immediately. Relocate him to a secure facility outside the Beltway. We suspect
the alien influence vector relies on physical proximity. We need to sever that
link."

Edith grimaced at the simplification. Michael ignored her.

"What's the second part?"

"Second recommendation. Reunite Todd with his family immediately. We believe
the daughter's collapse is a direct response to the denial of access. It's a
transaction. If we complete the transaction, we might wake the girl."

"Objectives?"

"De-escalation," Michael said. "It removes the provocation."

"Are you suggesting we provoked them, Lieutenant?"

"I'm suggesting that if Todd *feels* provoked, the tactical outcome is the
same."

Static hummed on the line.

"Your third point?"

"Intelligence. If the girl recovers, we establish a baseline for their
capabilities. We learn the rules of engagement."

"Stand by."

Michael punched the mute button. "Thoughts?"

"Stop calling it a theory," Edith snapped.

"I'm translating for a general, Edith. Let me sell it."

"Agreed," Rich said. "Let's hope they do the right thing."

*The military doesn't do the right thing,* Michael thought. *We do the
necessary thing.*

"Grant." Arnold was back. "The President authorized the move. We're moving the
family to the Beltway facility. Todd meets them there."

"Copy that, General."

"In the meantime, test your theory. I need leverage. Find me a weakness we can
exploit."

"Understood."

The line clicked dead.

Jennifer typed furiously. "I'm tagging the daughter's file. Any status change
hits my screen first." She offered a tight smile. "Nice work, Mike."

"He wants a weakness?" Rich stood up, pacing the small room. "That is suicide.
We should be establishing dialogue, not looking for a knife."

"It's an inventory, Rich," Michael said. "We need to know where we stand."

"We stand on an anthill," Rich countered.

Edith drummed her fingers on the table, calculating. "If you want a weakness,
start with the source."

"Meaning?"

"The President," she said. "He's hiding the contact method. Why?"

"He withheld that deliberately," Jennifer said. "We don't have the authority to
audit the Commander-in-Chief."

"He stalled," Edith corrected. "And now we're blind. If the contact method
contains a weakness, we need to see it."

"Is it even possible?" Rich asked.

Michael looked at Jennifer.

"We have the clearance," Edith said, answering for her. "We search executive
comms, NSA backchannels, priority overrides."

"Jennifer?" Michael asked.

She held his gaze. "I can dig it up. But the system watches the watchers, Mike.
Someone will know we looked."

"Can we do it?"

Jennifer hesitated. She wasn't looking at the screen anymore; she was looking
at the rank insignia on his chest. "I can try. But once I start, there is a
digital footprint. Even if I wipe it, a deep audit will find us."

She meant *court-martial*.

Michael looked at the dark screen of the secure phone. Arnold wanted
weaknesses. The President was hiding the catalyst. The girl was dying.
Following orders meant flying blind.

He looked back at Jennifer.

"Do it."
