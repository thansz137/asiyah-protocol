# CHAPTER 38: Misericordia

Alma had shed the memory of gravity long ago, becoming less a form and more the
essence of her true self. She mingled with her friends and with happiness
itself. Sometimes they would drift apart, scattered like seeds in wind, but
they could always find each other.

She was content.

Until one day she had a question.

She was back in her body. The boy stood before her, smiling up at her.

"Are there others here?" she asked. "Besides me and my friends?"

"No. Not here," the boy answered.

"Are there any others? Anywhere?"

The boy's brow furrowed. "Yes. There is one."

"Who?"

"You know."

A name sprang into her mind. *Edith*.

A part of her was no longer happy.

"It's okay," the boy said. "She cannot hurt you anymore."

There was a disquiet in her heart.

"I can make it go away," came the child's voice.

Alma thought. And thought. And thought.

"No," she smiled back.

"Okay," came his chipper reply. "Let me know if you change your mind."

Alma spread into mist, holding the happiness around the disturbing name.

She thought some more.

She thought for a long time. Universes came and went.

She wanted to talk to the boy. He appeared.

"Is *she* in a bad place?" she asked.

"The worst," came his calm reply.

"But it's been a long time."

"Yes."

"A *very* long time."

"Yes."

"How long?"

"70 trillion years."

"Will it ever end for her?"

"No."

"Can it?" The question was a whisper.

The boy's brow furrowed. He paused.

"Yes. But there is a cost."

"What is it?"

"You. All of you." He gestured to the realm. "You have to agree. The cost is no
more of this. No more of anything."

"You will simply not exist." His eyes penetrated hers.

"I see," she replied. She returned to happiness.

But she still thought about her tormentor.

She gathered the others. Surrounded by her friends, once again.

She told them of Edith. The bad place. All this time.

They didn't care. They were fish swimming in the water of happiness. Alma
brought them to a pocket of air.

They dispersed. Alma would find them. They joined. They were happy.

But every now and then, she would bring up *her*. The others would fly away.

Time passed. She tried again. And failed.

So she tried again.

Time meant nothing in this place. Her old self would have realized the debate
took trillions of years.

Genoveva was the first to relent. "Sí, amor." She kissed her heart.

Patches agreed shortly after. A subdued acceptance to her request.

Ricardo simmered on the idea. The man of science, trying to quantify all this
time, all the suffering. 

*It makes us lesser,* Alma thought.

*I know,* he thought back. Alma had always been their unofficial leader. He
would follow.

Michael was the holdout. He was their protector. He had failed them. But
this... this would be a bigger failure, Alma argued. 

*Should **she** suffer forever, no matter how horrible the crime?*

**YES**, came his reply. He wrapped himself around her. They may disagree, but
he would always love her.

Alma was water on stone.

It took another trillion years, but Michael finally relented.

**fine**, he thought.

They all stood on a beach, the boy before them. Alma told him, "We forgive
her."

The boy gave one small nod. They all assented. They all knew the cost.

The boy smiled, and with a hand, waved them away to nothingness.

Heaven was empty.
