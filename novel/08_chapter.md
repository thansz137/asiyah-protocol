# CHAPTER 8: The Cheget

**January 25, 1995**

The room was a coffin. Too small for so many men, too thick with sweat and
cigarette smoke to breathe easily. Walls the color of congealed fat reflected
the desk lamp in greasy streaks. Boris Yeltsin sat at the center of it all.
Slumped. Deflated. Cheeks flushed with fear and the dregs of a late night.

A black briefcase sat open before him, velvet and metal. Beside it. Three keys.
Three men. The mechanism of the world's end.

Across from him stood General Ivanov. Uniform immaculate. Voice trembling
beneath its bark. "Comrade President, the Olenegorsk radar confirms trajectory.
It's real. They fired."

Silence. Then the hum. The fluorescent lights. Unbearable.

Yeltsin blinked through the blur of exhaustion and disbelief. Fired. A Trident
missile. A single launch. Ten minutes to arrive. Seven of those minutes were
already gone.

He tried to recall his morning. A sharp throb at his right temple. A meeting
about bread shortages. An argument about privatization. Then pulled into this
room. Dragged from the living world. Into the one that ends it.

"Are you certain?" His voice cracked. A rasp dragged through alcohol and age.
"Not a weather rocket? An error?"

"Sir, we have confirmation from Olenegorsk and Missile Defense Command.
Trajectory is toward the mainland. Submarines have been alerted. The
retaliation protocol requires your..."

"Your key," another officer interrupted, holding out his hand. Fingers
trembling. A muscle jumping in his jaw. "Sir, we must prepare for a full
counterstrike."

Yeltsin's gaze dropped to the keys. Then the briefcase. It breathed in front of
him. The small red button pulsed like an open wound.

He thought of Reagan. Gorbachev. All the talk of trust and glasnost. How
quickly trust turned back into paranoia. He thought of his daughter. The stale,
cold smell of the Kremlin hallways.

If real, his people were dead. Already dead. If not? He would murder the world.

A bead of sweat rolled from his temple. Cold. He looked at his generals. Every
eye fixed on him. Waiting for the signal. For the key. Permission to erase
America.

"Maybe it's a mistake." He whispered to himself.

"Sir, there's no time."

"It must be a mistake."

The clock on the wall ticked. Louder. Hammering in the silence.

His hand hovered above the button. Trembling. He could feel its heat. The air
thickened. The room leaned forward. Waiting for the world to end.

He swallowed. Tasted iron. Bile. His mind was hollow.

"It must be a mistake," he said again. Louder this time.

*It must be a mistake...*
