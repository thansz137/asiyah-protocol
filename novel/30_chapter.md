# CHAPTER 30: Elohim

The drive upstate was a blur of rain-slicked highways and gnawing anxiety.
Jennifer pushed the rented sedan harder than she should have, the frantic call
from the residential home echoing in her mind. *Self-injurious behavior.
Escalating. Needs intervention*. Sarah. Her sweet, silent Sarah, trapped in a
world Jennifer couldn't reach, now turning that frustration inward. It was her
greatest fear, the one hidden burden she carried beneath the layers of
professional competence, the secret vulnerability that threatened to unravel
everything.

She pulled into the parking lot of the Maple Brook Care Facility, a low brick
building nestled amongst manicured lawns that seemed unnaturally green under
the grey, drizzling sky. She cut the engine and sat for a moment, gripping the
steering wheel, gathering the strength she lacked. She hated this place. Hated
the smell of disinfectant and quiet despair. Hated leaving Sarah here. But
there was no alternative. No family left, only her.

She forced herself out of the car and walked quickly towards the entrance,
nodding curtly at the receptionist. Down the familiar, sterile corridor, past
rooms where still figures stared blankly at flickering televisions. She reached
Sarah's door, Room 86, braced herself, and pushed it open.

And stopped.

The air hung still. Impossibly quiet. Usually, Sarah's distress filled the
small quarters, a palpable hum of agitation, of rhythmic rocking, of faint,
frustrated sounds. Now, there was only a profound stillness, a quality of
silence Jennifer had never felt here before. The harsh fluorescent overhead
light seemed softer, diffused, as if filtered through unseen layers of peace.

Sarah rested in her bed, tucked neatly under the thin blanket. Her face serene,
she did not rock or make a sound. She contemplated the window where rain traced
patterns on the glass.

And there was a man sitting beside her bed.

He was older, perhaps in his late seventies, wearing a simple dark coat over
unassuming clothes. A dark, plain yarmulke rested on his thin, gray hair.

He was holding Sarah's hand gently in both of his own, his head bowed slightly
as if listening or praying. His posture radiated a calm that emanated outwards,
filling the area. On the small nightstand beside him sat a battered, old
fashioned AM transistor radio and a pair of simple, white wired earbuds, coiled
neatly.

Jennifer's exhaustion vanished, replaced by a surge of protective fury. Who was
this stranger? How had he gotten in here? Why was he touching her sister?

"Who are you?" Jennifer demanded, her voice sharp, cutting through the
stillness. "What are you doing in my sister's room?"

The man looked up slowly. His countenance settled on hers. It was ancient,
kind, impossibly weary, yet held a spark of gentle amusement, a deep well of
peace that was entirely out of place in this sterile environment. He released
Sarah's hand carefully and rose slowly to his feet. He wasn't tall, but his
presence subtly altered the dimensions of the confined space, making it seem less
like a cage and more like a sanctuary.

"Forgive me," he said. His voice was soft, melodic, with a faint, unplaceable
accent. It wasn't just heard; it felt like a warm resonance in the air, calming
the frantic pounding in Jennifer's chest against her will. "I was walking down
the hall. I heard a distress. Such a deep sadness." He gestured vaguely towards
the corridor. "No staff was immediately available. Your sister, she was
agitated. I came in. I hoped perhaps I could offer some slight comfort. I have
a knack for it." He offered a small, hesitant smile that crinkled the parchment
of his skin.

Jennifer stood frozen, caught between suspicion and an undeniable, baffling
sense of peace radiating from him. Sarah turned her head on the pillow then,
looked directly at Jennifer, and smiled. A genuine, happy, uncomplicated smile.
A smile Jennifer hadn't seen in years.

Emotion rushed Jennifer's face, the figure of the old man blurring through the
tears. Her anger deflated, replaced by a wave of confusion and overwhelming
relief.

"She seems calm," Jennifer stammered, taking a step towards the bed.

"She has a beautiful spirit," the man said softly, his gaze resting on Sarah
with profound affection. "Just needed someone to sit with her in the quiet for
a moment. My name is Noah, by the way." He offered his hand.

Jennifer took it automatically. His grip was surprisingly firm, his skin dry
and cool, yet a gentle warmth seemed to flow from his touch, easing the tight
knot of anxiety in her stomach. "Jennifer Smith. Thank you, Noah. Truly. They
called me. Said she was agitated..." She trailed off, unable to voice the terror
fully.

She moved to Sarah's bedside, brushing a stray strand of hair from her sister's
forehead. Her look followed her, bright and aware, the earlier agitation
completely gone. Jennifer sank onto the edge of the visitor's chair Noah had
vacated, the exhaustion hitting her again, heavier this time now that the
immediate crisis seemed averted.

"They don't understand her here," Jennifer found herself saying, the words
tumbling out before she could stop them, addressed as much to the surrounding
stillness as to Noah. "They see the outbursts, the difficulties. They don't see
*her*. And with everything happening, the world feels like it's falling apart."
She gestured vaguely, referencing the chaos the public knew from the news, the
apprehension gripping everyone since the UN incident. "Trying to manage this
too, it's just," her voice broke, thick with unshed tears, "it's too much
sometimes. Falling all on me. Alone."

Noah pulled the other bedside chair closer, sitting opposite her, creating an
intimate island of calm amidst her storm. He listened patiently, his ancient gaze
holding hers with unwavering, gentle attention, offering no judgment, only
presence. The silence that followed her confession wasn't empty; it felt full,
understanding.

"May I offer a thought?" Noah asked quietly, after a long moment. Jennifer,
emotionally raw, exposed, simply nodded, too tired to refuse.

He leaned forward, his cadence dropping to a near hypnotic rhythm, the words
carrying a weight both ancient and immediate. "This *Asiyah*, this place of
action," he began, the Hebrew word sounding impossibly resonant on his tongue,
"it can be like a storm, Jennifer. A confusing, terrifying storm, especially
now. It demands so much. Your work demands everything. The news screams panic.
It pulls you outwards, scattering your strength, making you adrift, alone."

He paused, his gaze drifting to Sarah, who watched him with serene
concentration. "But the Creator," Noah continued, his words soft yet resonant,
"is not only in the grand design, the turning of galaxies, the unfolding of
history. The Creator is *here*. Now. Hiding within the action, within the
creation. Within this moment. Within your sister. Within you."

He returned Jennifer's gaze, holding it with gentle intensity. "You feel torn.
You feel the weight is too much. Because you are looking at the storm outside.
But the most important work, the *truest* work, is not out there. It is here."
He gestured towards Sarah. "This is your duty. Your sacred obligation. Not a
burden, but a privilege. To care for her. To protect her. To be present for
her."

His voice softened further, becoming almost a whisper, yet each word landed
with the force of revelation. "In all the chaos, Jennifer, amidst the fear and
the uncertainty, the plots of men and the whispers of things beyond our
understanding, do not lose sight of the one thing that truly matters. The one
thing that anchors us. The one thing that *is* real."

He reached out then, his cool, dry hand covering hers where it rested on the
bed rail. "Love," he whispered, the word itself seeming to shimmer in the air.
"Simply love. Love your sister. That is your strength. That is your purpose.
That is where you will find the Creator you seek. Not in the headlines. Not in
the clamor of the dying hour. But here. In the loving. That is all that is ever
asked of us."

Tears were streaming freely down Jennifer's face now, but they weren't tears of
sorrow or exhaustion. They were tears of release, of recognition, of a
profound, unexpected peace settling over her weary soul. It felt like coming
home after a long, brutal journey.

Noah gently took Sarah's hand again, then placed Jennifer's hand over her
sister's, enveloping both their hands within his own. The three of them sat
there, connected. Jennifer cried silently, letting the years of tension, of
fear, of loneliness wash away. Sarah watched her sister, her look bright, and
smiled again, a smile of pure, untroubled love. And Noah sat with them, a
silent, ancient guardian, holding their hands, radiating a peace that felt
older than time itself, a quiet light against the encroaching dark of the world
outside.
