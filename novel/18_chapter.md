# CHAPTER 18: The Calculus

The bathroom was cold. Michael gripped the porcelain, staring at the face in
the glass. It looked like a casualty report. Bloodshot eyes, skin like wet ash,
a jaw tight enough to crack molars. The bottle rattled as he shook two more
pills into his palm. He swallowed them dry, waiting for the chemical clarity to
burn off the fog.

He returned to the conference room. Three faces turned toward him, expecting
disaster.

"Michael?" Jennifer asked.

Michael dropped into his chair, the leather creaking in the silence. "The
President's daughter is responsive. She's going to make it."

Shoulders dropped. A collective exhale around the table.

"That's something, at least," Jennifer said.

"There's more," Michael said. "Most of the world's leaders are converging on
the UN tomorrow. They're going public. Full disclosure of contact, introduction
of the Envoys, everything."

"Wow." Jennifer looked at the wall monitor. "Telescope repositioning yesterday.
Emergency summit tomorrow. That is a hell of a timeline."

"It's a race," Michael said. "Political machinery moving in the dark. But you
can't hide a summit this size. It leaks. We're on a countdown."

"You said 'envoys,'" Edith said. She leaned forward, the exhaustion in her face
replaced by sharp curiosity. "Plural."

"Yes."

"We have confirmed them in other nations?"

"Yes."

"How did the President learn of them?"

Michael went still. He couldn't tell them the truth. The President acting on
dream-logic was not something you told a scientist you were trying to recruit.

"I can't answer that, Edith."

The camaraderie in the room evaporated. Rich looked down at the table. Edith
leaned back, crossing her arms.

"I see," she said. "I assume you also can't explain how those leaders knew to
reposition the telescopes before the signal arrived?"

"No," Michael said. "I can't."

"Of course." Her voice was ice. "I don't work in the dark, Lieutenant. I need
data."

"We all do," Jennifer said, her voice sharp. "But he has orders, Edith. You
know how the game is played."

"I know how you play it. I'm a scientist. If the variables are hidden, the
solution is flawed."

"The variables are classified," Jennifer countered.

"Enough," Michael said. He put his hands flat on the table, cutting the air
between them. "We don't have time for this. Edith, I can't tell you. That is
the job. But I promise you this." He locked eyes with her. "I will never lie to
you. I might refuse to answer. But I will not bullshit you. Ever."

Edith studied him. She didn't look away. She just nodded, accepting the terms
of engagement.

"Understood," she said. "Disappointing. But understood."

"Same here," Rich said.

"Good. Because we need to pivot." Michael leaned back. "Miles has been
extracted to the Beltway facility. The family is with him. We're moving to
phase two. Assessment."

"Interviews?" Rich asked.

"Direct contact," Michael said. "I'm on the shortlist. I'm waiting for the call
now."

The tension in the room shifted instantly from suspicion to adrenaline. Edith's
eyes widened. "You're going to speak with the Envoy? Directly?"

"That's the order," Michael said. "But I'm not going in there to chat about the
weather. I need ammunition. What do I ask him?"

"He claims he's here to stop us from annihilating ourselves," Rich said.

Edith frowned. "Why now? We've had nukes since '45. We almost ended it during
the Cuban Missile Crisis. Where were they then?"

"Maybe they were there," Michael said. "Arkhipov."

Jennifer looked up. "The Soviet sub commander? The one who refused the launch
order?"

"Exactly," Michael said. "October 27th, 1962. One man stopped nuclear war from
breaking out."

"Stanislav Petrov," Jennifer said, ticking a finger against the table. "1983.
The false alarm. He disobeyed protocol and refused to report the incoming
missiles."

"January 1995," Michael said. "The Norwegian Rocket incident. Yeltsin had the
nuclear briefcase open. The countdown was running. He decided not to push the
button."

Rich stopped tapping his pen. Edith stared at the wall monitor. The three dates
lay between them like evidence photos, the connection absolute.

"Three times," Edith said softly. "Three times the world should have ended. And
every time, one man simply... decided not to."

"We thought we were lucky," Michael said.

"And now you think we were managed," Edith said.

Rich rose to his full height, standing well above the others. "So it's possible
these aliens intervened in past events, but without announcing their
involvement. I think it would be good to try and find out if that was really
the case and if so, why now is different."

"If they're this advanced," Michael said, "why use Todd Miles? Why not appear
on the White House lawn? A pulsar signal proves they exist. Todd Miles just
proves we have a hostage situation."

"Because of the noise," Jennifer said.

"What noise?"

"Imagine General Arnold walks in here right now and orders you to deploy a
nuclear weapon."

"I'd confirm authentication codes," Michael said. "Then I'd do it."

"Now imagine he walks in wearing a bright red cocktail dress and lipstick,"
Jennifer said. "Same order. Same voice. Do you execute?"

Michael paused. "No. I'd call a medic."

"Exactly. The signal is the same, but the packaging corrupts the data. You
can't process the order because you're too busy processing the dress. An alien
form on the lawn is the red dress. It's too much noise. We'd panic. We wouldn't
hear a word they said."

"So Todd is the camouflage," Rich said.

"He's the interface," Edith corrected. She tapped the table. "Think about it,
Michael. You're smarter than a dog, right?"

"Most days."

"Could you explain the concept of a prime number to a golden retriever?"

"No."

"Not because you aren't smart," Edith said. "But because you don't share a
protocol. You don't have the hardware to transmit, and the dog doesn't have the
software to receive. These things? They aren't just smarter than us. They're
operating on an entirely different operating system. The fact that they figured
out how to port their data into a human brain so we could understand it? That
is the most terrifying part of this entire week."

"Why?" Michael asked.

"Because it means they have root access," Edith said. "They didn't just knock
on the door. They rewired the doorbell."

"We are outgunned," Michael said. "We need an edge. Edith. Arnold shut you down
on ADAM. He called it a broken machine. Why?"

"Because he's afraid of it," Edith said.

"What is it?"

"True AI. Built in '93 by Dr. Stephan Brown and my father."

Rich shook his head. "Strong AI doesn't exist. Not in '93. Not now."

"It exists," Edith said. "It's just in a cage."

"Why did Arnold kill it?" Michael asked.

"He didn't kill it. He buried it. The machine started learning the researchers
faster than they were learning it. It profiled them by syntax. My father caught
it first. He realized it knew who was typing. That terrified him. So he and
Brown wrote the Protocols. Total anonymity. No direct interface. All inputs
scrubbed and batched by committee."

"It blinded the system," Jennifer said.

"It kept it safe," Edith said. She looked down at her hands. "It didn't save my
father. He died in that lab. Massive heart attack. The stress just... broke
him."

The room went quiet. Michael watched her trace a grain in the wood table.

"Dr. Brown was devastated," Edith continued softly. "They weren't just
colleagues. Brown was my stepfather. When my father died, Brown fell apart. And
ADAM faltered right along with him. I don't know if the code relied on their
specific dynamic or if Brown just lost the capacity to maintain it. But shortly
after the funeral, the system went silent. It just stopped answering."

"So Arnold pulled the plug," Michael said.

"Arnold wanted a gun. Brown gave him a philosopher in solitary confinement. And
a broken one at that. So Arnold locked the door."

"And you think we should open it?"

"We are trying to understand an *extraterrestrial* alien intelligence," Edith
said. "I think our best bet is to try and fix the *terrestrial* alien
intelligence we already have."

The door opened. No knock. A Secret Service agent stepped inside, filling the
frame.

"Lieutenant Grant. We are green."

Michael stood up. The briefing was over. The Assessment was beginning.

He looked at the team. In an hour, they had gone from strangers to
conspirators. Edith looked ready to fight. Rich looked terrified but present.
And Jennifer...

She met his gaze. A sharp nod. *Bring it home.*

Michael turned to the agent. "Lead the way."
