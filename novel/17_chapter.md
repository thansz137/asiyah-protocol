# CHAPTER 17: The Star in the East

Michael followed General Arnold back to the sub-basement. The room was sterile.
It smelled of floor wax and recycled air. Arnold didn't sit. He stood by the
steel door, his body rigid, trembling with a suppressed rage that made the
small space feel like a pressure vessel.

"So, Lieutenant Grant. Your theory was correct. Congratulations."

"Thank you, sir."

"Do not get comfortable. Did your team find anything else?" The General pitched
his voice low, a quiet, granular rumble that barely escaped his throat.

Michael treaded carefully. "We audited the timeline, sir. We found the initial
executive orders to reposition the Fermi Telescope. We also found identical
movements from foreign assets. France. China. Russia." Michael met the
General's gaze. It was a wall of granite. "We found the *what*, General. But we
could not find the *why*. There was no intelligence chatter. No intercepts. Just
spontaneous, simultaneous orders from world leaders to look at the same patch
of empty sky."

Arnold went statue-still. He locked eyes with Michael, refusing to blink, his
stare drilling into the junior officer until Michael had to fight the urge to
look at the floor. The air in the room grew stagnant. Arnold took a step
closer, invading the personal space between them, and lowered his tone to a
flat, dead whisper.

"Since you were so thorough, let me tell you what precipitated the orders."
Arnold leaned in. The veins in his neck were cords of wire. "Your President had
a dream."

Michael blinked. "Sir?"

"A *dream*, Lieutenant. And he wasn't scared. He was driven. He woke up with a
singularity of purpose I have never seen in a man. He refused to tell me what
he saw. He just gave me the coordinates and ordered the movement." Arnold's
composure cracked. He slammed a hand against the metal table. "I am the only
one who knows the source. And now I am telling a green fucking lieutenant, just
so you understand exactly how fucking broken this situation is!"

A fleck of spittle flew from Arnold's lips. He glared at Michael, his chest
heaving as he fought to regulate his breathing.

"And he wasn't the only one." Arnold wiped his mouth with the back of his hand.
"Intel confirms other leaders fell into waking stupors or sudden sleep cycles
right before issuing identical orders. Paris. Beijing. Moscow. They are all
compromised."

Arnold took a step closer, his eyes wide and unblinking. "If they can reach the
Commander-in-Chief, they can reach anyone. The head of the NSA. The Joint
Chiefs. Even you, Lieutenant."

*Or yourself, General,* Michael thought. But he kept his face blank.

"After the signal arrived, the President started giving me intel on Todd
Miles," Arnold continued, pacing away. "He told me Miles had to be brought in.
He is acting on instructions I cannot vet, from a source I cannot see."

"Is he being guided? Is he being manipulated?" Arnold paced the small floor.
Three steps. Turn. Three steps. "Even if I knew he was compromised... what is
the protocol, Grant? Do I tell the American people we are removing the
Commander-in-Chief because voices in his head told him to move a telescope?
Voices nobody else can hear?"

Michael remained silent. There was no answer in the manual for this.

"Your plan to separate him from Miles was clever. But it was a band-aid on a
hemorrhage." Arnold stopped pacing. He looked at the ceiling, as if trying to
see through the concrete to the Oval Office above. "If they can reach him in
his sleep, proximity is irrelevant. Or perhaps there are other envoys. The
chef? The Secret Service detail? The nurse changing his daughter's IV bag?"

Arnold looked at his own hands, then clenched them into fists.

"Our entire apparatus is vulnerable to a threat we cannot see. We have the most
powerful military in human history, and I have no target. I cannot drone a
dream, Lieutenant. Our enemy is ethereal. They know our biology, our math, our
fears. We know nothing." Arnold's eyes locked onto Michael's. "That is not a
tactical disadvantage. That is checkmate."

Michael watched the General. This was a man who had signed deployment orders
for hundreds of thousands of soldiers. He had stared down nuclear brinkmanship.
But now, the granite was cracking. His hands, usually steady as stone, trembled
slightly as he adjusted his cuff links.

Outside this sealed room, the world was burning. Every major leader was
airborne. Intelligence agencies were chasing shadows. The press was minutes
away from breaking the story of the millennium. And in the center of the storm,
the highest-ranking military officer in the United States was confessing his
helplessness to a junior analyst.

Arnold took a breath that shuddered in his chest. He closed his eyes for a
second, and when he opened them, the soldier was back.

"The cat is out of the bag, Lieutenant. We are racing the news agencies now,
not just the aliens. We need to understand the message before the leak breaks
and global panic sets in." Arnold's voice was crisp again. Operational. "The
President is quarantined from Miles until the UN summit. In the interim, a
select group has been cleared for direct contact to conduct an assessment. You
are on that list, Grant."

Arnold stepped closer. "Find me an edge. I don't care how small. Find me
something I can use."

"Understood, sir."

"Return to your team. Stay ready. Someone will retrieve you for the Miles
facility." Arnold checked his watch, then fixed Michael with a final stare.
"You are cleared to discuss the interview. But the dream? The compromised
command?" Arnold opened the heavy steel door. "That conversation never
happened. Understood?"

"Yes, sir."

Arnold didn't acknowledge the confirmation. He shoved the door outward and
stepped through, his movements tight and coiled. He marched down the corridor
without looking back. The door swung shut, severing the connection with a heavy
metallic thud.
