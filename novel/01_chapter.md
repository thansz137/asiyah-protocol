# CHAPTER 1: Genesis

**Present Day The Pentagon, Arlington County, VA**

Lieutenant Michael Grant had a gift for seeing patterns others missed. Every
morning, his unit was responsible for summarizing overnight major intel for the
Pacific Asian region, and Michael's briefings were the ones commanders actually
read. Peering behind the curtain of commercial news sources, he saw a world on
fire. North Korean missile tests. Chinese naval exercises in the Taiwan Strait.
Border skirmishes the public never heard about. It had been a hectic night.
Most of them were.

As a West Point graduate, the lieutenant had spent some time in Iraq as an
Intel officer. Missions were created and soldiers deployed based on the
military assessments he authored. Educated guesses, most of the time, built
from incomplete intelligence and probability. Sometimes the missions paid off.
Many times they didn't. Either way, it was not unusual for some soldiers to pay
the ultimate price, while others sustained serious injury. Despite the
repercussions, his assessments had the highest accuracy rate in his unit. His
mind could untangle a web of seemingly unrelated facts into a narrative that
often matched the reality of the situation. Military intel was flawed by
nature, but his analyses gave soldiers the best chance of coming home. He was a
lowly lieutenant, but a carefully worded summary or a strategic comment could
nudge the top brass toward the right decision. Michael was able to cope with
the difficulty of his tour knowing that if he didn't do his job, someone else
would take his place. Someone less talented, or less lucky.

After his tour, Colonel Hayes called him into the office. "You're wasted out
here, Grant. Pentagon needs analysts who can think three moves ahead." Two
months later, he was still a lieutenant, but he was sitting in briefings with
generals.

Transferring from a combat zone where his intel saved lives to the
air-conditioned corridors of the Pentagon was difficult. Despite his
reservations, he was able to adapt and learn his new role. The webs were made
of different patterns, but he soon learned the new strands and became a trusted
advisor for his commander.

This morning's summary was almost ready. He was making some final adjustments
and proofreading when his phone rang. Caller ID was blocked. Oddly, he'd never
seen it blocked before. He picked up the receiver. "Lieutenant Michael Grant
speaking."

His commanding officer's voice came through. "Mike, this is Major Pardo. Come
down to my office immediately. Bring your full uniform." A soft click indicated
the call was over.

He ran a hand along his jaw, grabbed his jacket and hat, then walked past
cubicle walls to the major's office. He was raising his hand to knock on the
open door when he heard the major call, "Come in. Close the door and have a
seat."

Michael took the chair facing the desk. Major Pardo could often be curt, but he
sensed something was off. The major hadn't looked at his computer once. His jaw
was tight, and when he spoke, his voice had a careful flatness to it.

"Michael, there are military police coming to this office. You will be escorted
to the parking garage, where General Arnold will meet you." He waited for
Michael's reaction.

Michael paused. General Arnold. The four-star general who led the military
intelligence community at the Pentagon. An MP escort just to meet with him?

"Yes, sir." The words came after a beat of silence.

"Meet the MPs at our unit's door. Do not enter the hallway. You are dismissed."

Michael stood at attention and saluted. The major held his gaze, stood, and
returned the salute. The lieutenant turned and walked to the office doors.
Coworkers offered friendly smiles as he passed. A few called out greetings. He
nodded back but kept moving. As he reached the office entrance, the doors
opened and two MPs walked in.

"Lieutenant Grant?" "Yes."

"We are to escort you to the parking garage immediately. Please follow us." One
MP stepped aside, letting Michael through the unit doors. The other moved ahead
to lead them through the hallway. The three of them travelled down the halls
in a single line—one MP in front, one behind, and Grant in the middle, slightly
taller than the men escorting him. He could feel the curious stares as he was
marched through the Pentagon, not in handcuffs but clearly being escorted.
While somewhat embarrassing, the walk gave him time to think.

A four-star general. MP escort. No briefing. The pieces added up to something
urgent. Important enough to pull a lieutenant from his desk without warning. 

*Let the general talk*, he told himself. *Only ask questions if there is time
and if they are pertinent.*

The trio arrived at the parking deck, where a line of vehicles stood at the
ready. A civilian police car idled at the head of the line. A military Humvee
was right behind, followed by a black SUV. Another Humvee completed the escort.
Michael was directed to the open back door of the SUV, where a man in a dark
suit and earpiece stood waiting.

He ducked his head to enter the vehicle. General Arnold sat on the other side,
watching the lieutenant in silence as he awkwardly climbed into the back seat.

"Sir!" Michael brought his hand up in a brisk salute.

"At ease, Lieutenant."

As he settled himself in, the door closed behind him. The suited man walked
around to the front passenger seat and climbed in. The agent spoke into his
wrist, "Move out." The line of cars drove off, the sirens of the lead squad
echoing within the confined space of the parking garage.

The general turned to Michael. "Lieutenant, we are taking you to Reagan
National Airport. There you will board a military jet that will take you to
your destination. You will be briefed in-flight. I won't go into detail here.
It's better for you to get everything on the plane. This is a fluid situation.
You are to be my eyes and ears on the ground. Your role will be strictly
observational. Do you understand?" 

"Yes, sir." Michael responded.

"Good. I'll need your cellphone for now."

Michael fished a hand down the inside of his jacket. He produced a cell phone
and handed it to the General.

The escort had reached the highway, minutes from the airport. Michael could see
that the highway had been cleared from civilian traffic.

The general pocketed the phone, studying Michael like he was reading a report.
"Let's be clear, Lieutenant. I didn't pick you."

Michael's brow furrowed slightly, but he remained silent.

"I submitted a request to the Homeland Analytic Cell," Arnold continued, his
voice low and frustrated. "Asked for an analyst with a field background. The
kind of profile I've used a dozen times. They didn't give me a list. They gave
me a name. Yours."

Michael's stomach dropped. The HAC? Their work was whispered among his peers,
a faceless, bureaucratic black box that no one was allowed to question. "Why
me, sir?"

"That's the part that gives me a headache." The general's expression soured.
"They didn't flag your combat record. They flagged a theoretical analysis you
wrote. A 'future-threat' paper. You were supposed to be summarizing a DARPA
briefing on biomimetic processing, and you went off on a tangent about
'non-mammalian intelligence models.' Ring a bell?"

"Sir, I... that was just a blue-sky analysis. I saw a potential threat model in
their research, something I thought might be applicable in the future. The far
future."

"Doesn't matter." Arnold interrupted, "The HAC's threat model flagged your
analysis. It labeled you a 'cognitive outlier.'"

The general leaned in, his eyes locking with Michael's. "You've been on their
'persons of interest' file ever since. Now, your number has been called. So, as
I said, lose any preconceptions. Take everything in. Your role is
observational. But they think you'll see something the rest of us won't."

General Arnold eased back, his gaze turning to the window as if searching for
something. Michael stared at his own hands. A cognitive outlier. They'd been
watching him.

"Remember, son," Arnold said, his tone grim, "don't disappoint your country."

"Yes, sir," Michael replied. *Don't disappoint your country? No pressure*, he
thought.

The entourage arrived at the airport. The line of vehicles was waved through a
chain-link fence and brought to a hangar. The general's eyes rarely left
Michael as an uncomfortable silence settled in the car. Waiting in the hangar
was a sleek jet with its stairway down, two camouflaged marines standing on
either side. The SUV circled around the jet, stopping before the marines.

The agent in the passenger seat exited, spoke into his wrist, and opened the
back door. The general walked up to one of the marines and turned, waiting for
Michael to follow. Michael saluted and stood at attention. The general
returned the salute, his eyes fixed on the young lieutenant.

With that, Michael marched past the marines and up the stairway. As he entered
the jet, there was only one thought occupying his mind. 

*What the hell have I just gotten into?*
