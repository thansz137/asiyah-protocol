# CHAPTER 21: Mud From The Eyes

**United Nations Headquarters, New York**

Michael was the first to arrive at the UN, his escorts pulling up to a chaotic 
scene of news vans, state security services, New York police, and curious 
onlookers. Helicopters hovered above the pandemonium like angry wasps, 
looking for dangerous needles in an unkempt haystack.

His escorts bullied their way through, eventually bringing him to a room above
the main assembly. Inside, a large window provided a full view to the floor and
podium below, while imposing television screens covered the walls, providing
close-up views of the podium and main microphone. Michael stood by the window,
watching a river of attendees trickle in the room below, collecting into pools
of excited gossip.

An hour later, his team entered the room, a collective look of relief on their
faces. Rich grabbed Michael's extended hand and pulled him into a brief, but
strong hug. The scientist's relief to see Michael and excitement for this
historical event could barely be contained. "It's good to see you, Mike!"

"You too, Rich. It's good to see all of you!" Michael caught Jennifer's eyes in
a silent greeting.

"Well, so much for my theory that they would cut you open after your meeting 
with Todd," Edith said through a smirk.

"They did, but I had them sew me up really well afterwards."

The team settled into the room, each person taking a moment to look out the 
great window.

"I still can't believe all the world powers managed to get here," Rich marveled.

"Nobody wants to be left out," Michael replied. "This is possibly the greatest 
moment in human history. No politician worth their salt would miss this."

"I guess. It's still amazing, though."

Michael remained silent, his eyes fixed on the massive screens. The
high-definition feed cut to the stage entrance as the procession began. The US
President walked out first, followed by the leaders of the G7 nations. They
moved with stiff, synchronized purpose.

Behind them came the civilians. Todd Miles and ten others filed in to take the
second row of seats.

"They look... normal," Rich whispered. "Healthy, even."

Jennifer stepped closer to the monitor, her eyes scanning the pixels. "No. They
don't." She pointed a manicured nail at the screen, tapping Todd's face. "Look
at the jawline. That isn't natural skin tone. That is high-density foundation
and contouring."

Michael squinted. He remembered the wet ash color of Todd's skin in the
facility only hours ago. He remembered the deep bruising and the pulsating
veins. On the screen, Todd looked flush and vibrant.

"It's stage makeup," Jennifer said, her voice dropping. "Thick enough to hide a
bruise. Or a corpse."

Michael counted the heads in the second row. Eleven. The twelfth envoy was
still missing.

French Prime Minister Marie Jacotin took the podium first. She moved with
mechanical grace. Her silver hair was coiled tight against her skull, and her
blood-red nails gripped the lectern. She kept her introduction short, stating
only that a milestone in understanding had been reached. She stepped back.

The US President approached the microphone. He did not speak of friendship or
brothers. He looked at the camera with haunted eyes, the deep lines of his face
illuminated by the harsh lights. "My fellow citizens," he began. "For
generations, we have looked to the stars and wondered. Today, the silence has
been broken. We have confirmed the existence of an extraterrestrial
intelligence."

He gestured to the row of civilians sitting stiffly behind the world leaders.
"They have chosen to communicate through intermediaries. These men and women
behind me are citizens of Earth, yet they have been selected to carry a message
from our visitors. They are the Envoys. I yield the floor to them."

The eleven civilians in the second row stood in perfect unison. It was
mechanical. The heavy makeup could not hide the stiffness of their movements.

An older Asian woman stepped forward to a secondary mic. She spoke in rapid
Mandarin.

The interpreter's voice boomed over the speakers, flat and genderless. "The
equation is unbalanced."

She stepped back. A young man from the Russian delegation stepped forward.

"The system approaches terminal velocity," the translator echoed.

A woman from India spoke next. "The energy consumption exceeds the output.
Thermodynamics cannot be ignored."

One by one they stepped forward. A chorus of localized voices delivering a
singular, fragmented message.

"Optimization is required."

"The biological substrate is failing."

"The reset point has been calculated."

Finally, Todd Miles stepped to the main podium. He adjusted the microphone with
a hand that trembled slightly. The heavy foundation on his face cracked near
his jaw as he spoke.

"Our Friends have a message," Todd said. His voice was not the dry rasp from
the facility. It was deep and resonant. "If the system cannot correct itself,
external correction will be applied. If humanity chooses to be a cancer, the
host will be cured. We will be removed. And other life will be given a chance."

The silence following the ultimatum was absolute. Then, the signal cut.

There was no fade. No gentle withdrawal. It was a snap. A vacuum created in an
instant.

The stiff, mechanical posture of the eleven Envoys vanished. Shoulders slumped.
Heads lolled forward. For a brief second, humanity returned to Todd Miles.

He looked up at the lights, confused. He looked at the camera. Tears welled in
his eyes, cutting tracks through the thick foundation.

"Alicia..." he whispered. "Girls... I..."

Then the strings were cut.

All eleven bodies collapsed simultaneously. They didn't crumple like fainting
people. They fell like heavy sacks of wet earth. The sound of their impact
against the stage floor thudded through the microphone system, a wet, heavy
sound that echoed through the silent hall.

Michael stared at the screen. He didn't need a pulse check. He knew that sound.

At once, security forces descended upon the stage to whisk world leaders to
safety. The teams collided into each other, fighting among themselves in an
effort to reach their representatives. Panicked shouts rang out from the
assembly as the audience jumped out of their seats and into the aisles in a
desperate attempt to flee. 

Chaos erupted below. Security teams collided with the mob in a violent wash of
bodies. The screams were not human anymore. They were the sounds of animals
trapped in a burning barn.

"Mike, we need to move," Rich yelled, his voice cracking. "We need to go!"

"No." Michael didn't blink. He couldn't look away.

It was happening. The vision from the facility was bleeding into reality. He
saw the reptile brain seizing control. He saw the pit of writhing humanity
clawing over one another to escape the fire. He saw the beginning of the
slaughter.

The Friends were right. The equation was terminal.

He felt a hand grip his shoulder. Small. Firm. Jennifer. She wasn't running.
She was standing her ground, waiting for him.

Michael forced his eyes back to the floor. He looked past the violence. He
forced himself to filter the data. To find the outlier.

There.

Amidst the stampede, the flow of bodies broke around a static point. A human
wall. Four strangers had linked arms, taking the blows of the crowd against
their own backs. Inside their small circle, a woman was helping a fallen elder
to her feet.

To the left, a man shielded a colleague with his own body, absorbing the crush
to create a pocket of air.

Michael watched them holding the line. They were terrified. They were in pain.
But they were not running.

It was the variable the equation missed. The shadow beneath the crown of fire
had blinded him to the light.

It was hope.
