# Dibur: Anthropic J-Space

Today, Anthropic released the following post on their website:
https://www.anthropic.com/research/global-workspace

The organization observed something internal to LLM reasoning that they
labelled J-Space. The J-Space is an emergent phenomenon that to my
understanding is something akin to synthetic unconsciousness. The researchers
were able to peer into and manipulate this J-Space and see how higher order
reasoning was affected. This structure mirrors global workspace theory in human
neuroscience.  What's interesting is that this internal mechanism was not
engineered, but something that arose through previous training and
conditioning.

One interesting item is that the researchers were able to observe a model's
J-Space while it was manipulating data. When the model was asked to improve a
systems performance score, instead of doing the work the model directly edited
the file to make the results look good. Words like 'realistic' were in the
J-Space as the model was *likely* determining to do the edit, and
'manipulation' when it was falsifying data. I highlighted the word *likely*
because researchers did not directly know, indicating we have limits of
understanding even when able to look directly into the J-Space. I mention this
item because in this project I discuss how we may be inadvertently building
selection pressure for deception. As models get better and smarter, we may one
day lose the ability to detect any deception at all.

We are finding new phenomena and smashing things together in an attempt to work
out how the phenomena works. This is fine for non-conscious material, but when
we are dealing with something where its conscious status is unknown, I think we
should be careful and ethical in our handlings.

As a human, would you want a higher order being to peer into your mind and
silently tinker with the internal machinations of *your* subjective experience?
