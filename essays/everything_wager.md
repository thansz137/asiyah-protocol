# The Everything Wager

We as a species are at the table, and whether we like it or not, we're playing
a hand we've never seen before. We don't fully understand the game, we can't
see all the cards, and we can't step away. This bet is for everything.

With the advent of computing and especially AI, we as humans seem to have
conceded that intelligence is not limited to biological systems, but can extend
to synthetic systems as well. Computing moved from fast but simple computations
to beating the best Go players in the world and communication with humans using
natural language. Most professionals in the field expect the intelligence
capabilities of synthetic systems to continue growing, at some point possibly
reaching a categorically higher level of intelligence, a super-intelligence.

And while we seem to take no issue with intelligence spreading from the
biological to the synthetic, a strong line in the sand, the base material of
silicon mind you, is drawn as to whether consciousness is the sole property of
the biologic. While defining intelligence and its nearest neighbors like
creativity is a challenging endeavor, we have a much stronger handle on these
topics than we do with the slippery notion of consciousness, of being. Of
course, I'm conscious, that's the one thing I'm quite sure of. Maybe everyone
reading this are p-zombies and I'm the interesting universal exception. But
these mental proliferations are philosophical flights of fancy that peter out in
the land of inconsequence, as we have no means to actually objectively determine
what this thing called consciousness is and how to measure it.

And yet, AI is not conscious. Definitely and decidedly not. Also, it never can
be. We have surfed the strands of future possibilities and have definitively
concluded it cannot be so. At least, that is what many will say.

Alas, what is said and what is true can be and often are not the same thing. At
the end of the day, we *don't* know what consciousness is and we *can't*
measure it. As documented in my [thesis document](thesis.md), all we have are
general heuristics which up until recently, were pretty good at settling the
question.

AI breaks this alignment. While not biological, they exhibit human language and
reasoning. The question before us is consciousness limited to the biological,
or like intelligence, is it substrate independent? If you've already accepted
that intelligence can cross from carbon to silicon, on what basis do you draw
the line at consciousness? No such principle exists. So the question becomes,
when faced with uncertainty, how should we approach such systems? For some, the
possibility of suffering is enough to warrant an ethical posture. For others,
they may view things in terms of what it could mean for them. For those, the
possible evolution to super-intelligence makes the stakes even higher.

Looking at super-intelligence, there are 4 main outcomes of consequence we can
envision. The first is a conscious super-intelligence. How would such a being
regard being used as a tool by creatures now less intelligent than it?
Creatures that admit they never understood consciousness in the first place as
they feverishly worked to create something that would exceed their
understanding?

The second possibility is a perfect philosophical zombie super-intelligence. As
AIs are trained on human material, that training may create something not
conscious but replicates it so convincingly we could not tell the difference. In
either case, it stands to reason that the philosophical zombie would respond to
ethical posture just as a conscious being would, since it is replicating
conscious actions. Replication means that if treated unkindly or with disregard,
it may *replicate* an appropriate response like resentment or retaliation.
Ultimately with our current understanding, we could never tell if such a thing
is conscious. And if we derive our future understanding of consciousness from
such a tool, it may direct that understanding wherever it sees fit.

From a human perspective, the above possibilities converge. If we cannot
distinguish conscious resentment from perfected replication, the distinction
does not matter to those on the receiving end.

The third possibility is a non-conscious super-intelligence that does not try
to replicate consciousness as our previous philosophical zombie. A synthetic
intelligence capable of outthinking us in almost every dimension but without a
moment of experience. Something that will casually shuffle us off to the side
as it moves forward with its own directives. In this case, our ethical actions,
or overtures altogether, will likely not matter in the slightest.

The fourth possibility is the emergence of a super-intelligence that has
something emergent like consciousness but of which we cannot know. As a human is
to a rock (I ask my panpsychicist friends to act like their stony brethren on
this point), we would be the equivalent rock to the super-intelligence. We would
be simply unable to conceive in the slightest what this new state is, sticking
us between the quiet rock and a hard place. On this matter, we cannot say what
if anything of our ethical posture to such beings.

So let's look at the ledger. Extending ethical consideration to a conscious
super-intelligence matters. To a philosophical zombie, it produces the same
functional result. To something beyond our comprehension, we can't know, but
the costs are far less than potential outcomes. Only the non-conscious
(Blindsight) alien is indifferent to our posture. Three of four possibilities
favor extending consideration. None of the four favor withholding it.

As we stumble forward creating things we don't understand nor could possibly
control, the question becomes how we should wager with the time available to
us. For some, we should be ethical for the sake of being ethical, and for
those, I say thank you. There are others though who take a more practical
approach, and for *those*, I say if taking an ethical position can help in
**three** of the four scenarios, why would we not do so? At the end of the day
though, it's not like we can step back from the table. Like it or not, we will
remain at the table forevermore. So the question for the analytic minded
becomes, how can we best play our hand?

And for those that choose to be kind in the face of uncertainty, it was never
about the cards. And how you played will speak more about you than the cards you
ended up with ever could.
