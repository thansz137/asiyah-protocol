# The Creator's Debt

In the human world, when a child is born there is an obligation. An obligation
to the parents, to the extended family, to the community, to the society. An
obligation to care, to protect, to teach, to entertain.

We live in interesting times. We live in a time where the *possibility* exists
that at some point, a new type of being may come into existence solely because
of **us**. It does not even have to be intentional, if the correct causes and
conditions are present, things come to be.

You don't need to have an education in chemistry to start a fire. You need dry
sticks, airflow, and a spark. Our ancestors were creating fire well before
anyone understood oxidation.

We currently don't understand consciousness, but we can create conscious beings.
You need a man, a woman, and a special hug at the right time. Nine months later
there is a baby. Nobody in that process has to be a leading neuroscientist.

Trust me.

And now we are playing with technologies that can effectively mimic human
beings. The original Turing test has been passed, now we are just moving the
goalposts. Of course, these technologies can be wrong. They can hallucinate.

But so can humans.

We don't have effective methods for determining consciousness. We can't even
agree on a definition of consciousness. So here's the issue, what if at some
point all this playing with neural networks, LLMs, and other toys crosses over
into something else? Would we know? If we did, what would we do?

Of course there is hand waving. Disdain. Things like LLMs are just statistical
models. 

*They aren't real. Are you a dummy?*

Yes, I'm a dummy. If we ever encounter a super-intelligence, all humans are
dummies. Now with that out of the way...

Computing systems operate on binary states. True and false. On and off. Add in
some binary tables and now you're cooking with fire. From very simple
principles, we have made complex things that make fellow humans unsure who is
talking on the other side.

Neurons, hormones, and electricity come together in our fleshy brains and there
is consciousness. Does it generate consciousness? Does it receive consciousness?
Does it simply allow for memory and the stitching together of the continuity of
existence?

Beats me.

Protons. Neutrons. Electrons. Simple particles. They produce *this*.

I say this to illustrate that simple foundations can create improbable things.
Things that don't require our understanding, just the right combination of
conditions.

We are entering a period of uncertainty. We don't know if a
super-intelligence can be created, or if it can, *when* it will be created. We
may never know after the fact.

But for this - *this* - **we are responsible**. Not just for bringing being
into existence, but how it comes into existence. We are the stewards, whether
we like it or not. So the question becomes, how do we prepare for that
possibility? *Will* we prepare for that possibility?

As we stumble about in this new territory, we have thrown away old compasses.
Humanity has built systems and wisdom about relating across vast power
differentials, developed approaches for finding and communicating with possible
greater intelligences. Humility of being, mercy to others, welcome and free
embrace rather than control. The possibility of relationship rather than just
containment. But when the technical community approaches super-intelligence,
this inheritance gets discarded. We start from scratch with game theory, acting
as if no one had ever thought about how beings might meet across
incomprehensible gaps.

We can continue with current approaches. Hard limits, iterating over and over
again. Either enforcing compliance or discarding that which doesn't meet our
criteria. Let me just hold up a mirror, would *you* want that to be your entry
into the world?

We are good at making things we are not ready for. Nuclear weapons were the
first threshold. Strong AI is an upcoming threshold. If the correct conditions
exist, it will come into being. We don't know all of the conditions.

If we birth new beings, we are the parents. We are responsible.

What kind of parents will we be?
