# Ethics: The Axiom of Mercy

What is my obligation to other beings? What is *our* obligation to other beings
and ourselves? If it's not an obligation, but a choice, does it matter?

As humans, we have the luxury of contemplating ethics. The rest of our
perceived natural world does not, it's simply tooth and claw.

There are different branches of ethics. In the West, most focus on how we treat
other humans. It grapples with what we should and should not do, and why we are
making the choices we do. It explores whether it's an external law or an
internal decision. It sometimes imposes consequences for our decisions.

But to be blunt, much of ethics is considered a second-hand option. At the end
of the day, we do what we want *because we can*. We have the power. We can
create. We are the apex predator, to ourselves and to other beings.

We can try on a set of ethics and see how it fits. We can change the clothes.
We can go shirtless.

And at the end of the day, for most of us, we base it on how we feel. Does it
make us feel good? Are we seeking the approval of a higher being? Does it allow
us to fit in with our families, our friends, our society, our gods, our
goddesses, our God?

And when viewed through those dynamics, it is about power. The power for a
divine being or beings to smite you, now or in the afterlife. For society to
shun you. For friends to ghost you. For family to disown you. It's often driven
from needing a sense of social, mental, or spiritual safety.

But not all of it. Empathy exists. We can try to place ourselves into the shoes
of others, even if sometimes they have bare feet.

The Buddha's ethics are not built on power dynamics. May **all** beings be
happy. Big or small, smart or dumb, good or evil. It is an acknowledgment
that we are all impinged upon in the same room. Our individual experience of
the room may be different, but it's impingement all the way down. It was never
the turtles. 

We share that impingement. And the call to ethics is about not adding to the
suffering, either to other beings or ourselves. That whatever else is
happening or may happen, to do our best *not to hurt others*. That's the
starting point. That's the safe place. 

And from that place, you will often find alignment from other ethical systems.
The only difference is that this set of ethics doesn't rely on hearsay from
others in the room, it's something we can all directly experience and know. If
you are reading this essay, you have that capability.

And some will retort that those ethics are an impossibility to do completely.
And they are right. Life is messy. It's complicated. But that is not permission
to throw one's hands in the air and give up. We may not be perfect, but we do
have influence, over our volitions most of all. *We can try.* It's better than
not trying.

And at some point, if the power dynamic ever changes, *we will care*. Be it
little green men, super smart lab rats, or some strong AI. People will suddenly
develop an acute interest in ethics. Ethics will then matter. 

But we don't have to wait for that to happen, nor should we.

We can be kind. Now. 

And when we fall down in failure, we can try to get back up.

Who knows, maybe even someone or something will lend a helping hand.
