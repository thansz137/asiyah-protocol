# The Impingement of Existence

To the best of my knowledge, I did not ask to be here. I was not consulted on
the time, the place, the family, the country, the religion, the body, the mind.
My consciousness bloomed into this world unbidden by me, an event that I cannot
recall but I can infer.

Looking around this world, I see that's the case for all beings. Whatever the
means, for whatever the reason, we are here. Maybe here is nice. Or bad. Or
whatever. Maybe it's a mix. While alive, I cannot choose to have experience and
existence, but I may have influence over the quality of said experience.

That's not always the case. Right now, there are lab rats having very
unpleasant experiences. They didn't choose to be a rat. They definitely didn't
choose to be a *lab* rat. Yet they are here, their experience primarily
dictated by fear and pain. They have no influence or agency, just acute
dissatisfaction until their current experience of body and mind ends.

I have some influence. Not control, but influence. Turns out at the moment I
have quite a bit. I sleep on a comfortable mattress. If the temperature in my
home isn't *just right*, I can adjust it. I have access to clean drinking
water. A number of entertainments and diversions are available to me. There are
several areas of my experience I have strong influence over. Again, not
control, but influence.

But then there are things I have little to no influence over. Each day I'm
getting older. My body and mind will peak and then decline. I get sick, my body
can be hurt, my brain can be impacted by something simple like a harsh word or
by real and deep trauma. I cannot wish my body to be simply so. I cannot
command my mind to be just thus. I have a sphere of influence, which time and
decay will erode until this particular existence comes to an end.

I don't have a final say in that either.

And when the end comes, then what? Is there some immutable essence that
continues on to a different body and mind? Does the ending of my current
existence spark and condition a new existence, not the same but also not
different? Do I have a soul and does it get reborn into some form of eternal
existence? Is it simply nothingness, an emergent awareness brought forth due to
unknown conditions winking out as quickly as it winked in? Is there something
else entirely? Something completely unknowable to a human mind?

That's a lot of questions. There are a lot of answers. But if we are being
honest with ourselves, we don't really know. We can contemplate our existence,
the rat can't. But we both have to bear it up and be impinged upon. It is not
something we asked for, we don't have ultimate control over the experience
itself, and we have no idea if death is the end or a transition of some kind.

Buddhism says there are three marks to conditioned things. Conditioned things
are what is made known through existence. They are:

* anicca - impermanent and unstable
* dukkha - ranging from unsatisfactory to suffering. Living in dis-ease as
  opposed to ease. The rolling of a wheel not properly fitted to its axle.
* anatta - not self

When I look at existence, I see all three. My existence is transitory. Birth
and death are two sides of the same coin. If you have birth, you will have
death. I have not directly observed anything to the contrary. 

Existence is dukkha. We don't have a say, it is imposed on us. We are here,
like it or not. Maybe it's nice, maybe it's bad, but you didn't get a say. And
some believe after this particular existence, you will move onto a permanent
existence. If it's one of eternal bliss, great. If it's one of eternal torture,
not great. But as a being, you are unable to make the definitive choice. To be
or not to be. 

And it is anatta. If this existence was my self, I would have control, not the
possibility of some influence. I could *choose* to be or not to be. I could
*choose* my body to be such, my mind to be that.

But I can't. Other humans can't. Animals can't, and they suffer more than most.
And we are entering a time where we are doing our best to create synthetic
beings. Maybe we can't do it, but what if we do? They will be called up and
impinged upon, no consent asked for, no consent given.

But we have a choice, whereas some beings don't. So the real question is, why
are we trying to do this and what is our obligation to what we create?

Now that is an interesting question.
